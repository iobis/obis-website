<h1><span class="usecase-icon-badge"><i class="bi bi-book" style="color: var(--tags);"></i></span>Inclusive Knowledge</h1>

OBIS values and integrates all knowledge systems, from advanced genomics to Indigenous and local ecological knowledge. By actively supporting data mobilisation in underserved regions and building local capacity for biodiversity data management, OBIS works to ensure that the global marine biodiversity record is as complete, equitable, and representative as possible.

*Supports: KMGBF Target 21, IPBES, BBNJ capacity-building provisions*

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Building local capacity to mobilise underrepresented biodiversity data in Latin America and the Caribbean</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-implemented">Implemented</span></div>
  OBIS has delivered targeted capacity-building programmes across Latin America and the Caribbean, training researchers, data managers, and institutions in Darwin Core standards, data quality control, and OBIS publishing workflows. These efforts have substantially increased the volume and diversity of data contributed by historically under-represented regions to the global biodiversity record.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Latin America and the Caribbean</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Biodiversity data from the Global South is disproportionately absent from global datasets — not because the biodiversity is absent, but because local institutions lack the technical capacity and infrastructure to mobilise it.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Substantially increased data contributions from historically under-represented countries, improving the geographic completeness of the global marine biodiversity record.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS publishing workflows, Darwin Core training resources, regional node support</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Crediting indigenous and local ecological knowledge within the global biodiversity record</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  OBIS has developed approaches for documenting and crediting Indigenous and local ecological knowledge within dataset metadata, recognising community observations as a legitimate and valuable component of marine biodiversity data and ensuring that traditional knowledge holders receive appropriate attribution.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global, with priority in Pacific Island States, Arctic communities, and coastal indigenous territories</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Indigenous and local ecological knowledge holds centuries of marine biodiversity observation that remains outside the formal scientific record, both because of technical barriers and lack of appropriate attribution frameworks.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> A more equitable and complete biodiversity record that recognises the contributions of knowledge holders and integrates diverse observation traditions into global data infrastructure.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS metadata standards, dataset attribution frameworks, community-contributed occurrence records</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Accommodating diverse observation types within standardised biodiversity data infrastructure</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  OBIS metadata standards have been extended to accommodate complex, heterogeneous data assets — including images, acoustic records, genetic sequences, and linked environmental data — enabling richer, more contextually grounded biodiversity records that reflect the full diversity of modern observation approaches.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Marine biodiversity data increasingly comes in forms — images, eDNA sequences, acoustic signals — that do not fit neatly into traditional occurrence record formats, creating barriers to integration and interoperability.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> A more inclusive and flexible OBIS infrastructure capable of ingesting and linking diverse data types, increasing the richness and completeness of the global biodiversity record.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> Darwin Core extensions, OBIS metadata standards, linked data frameworks</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Mobilising biodiversity data from under-represented ocean regions in Africa</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-potential">Potential use case</span></div>
  Expanding OBIS capacity-building activities across the African continent represents a major opportunity to mobilise vast untapped biodiversity data from one of the world's most under-represented ocean regions, strengthening both the global database and local scientific capacity to monitor and manage marine resources.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Sub-Saharan Africa, Indian Ocean coast, West African seas</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> African marine ecosystems are among the most data-poor in the global biodiversity record, limiting their representation in international assessments and their inclusion in conservation planning.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Substantially richer marine biodiversity coverage for African waters, supporting both the global record and local capacity to manage marine resources sustainably.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS publishing infrastructure, capacity-building programmes, regional node development</div>
  </div>
</div>
