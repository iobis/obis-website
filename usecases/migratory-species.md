<h1><span class="usecase-icon-badge"><i class="bi bi-arrow-left-right" style="color: var(--tags);"></i></span>Migratory Species &amp; Connectivity</h1>

OBIS reveals critical ecological corridors for whales, turtles, fish, and other migratory marine species, supporting international cooperation on conservation across jurisdictions. By aggregating occurrence data from tracking studies, research surveys, and institutional collections, OBIS provides the cross-border spatial picture that migratory species management demands.

*Supports: CMS, BBNJ Agreement, KMGBF Target 4*

<br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Mapping migratory corridors to design ecologically coherent cross-border MPA networks</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-implemented">Implemented</span></div>
  Within the MPA Europe project, OBIS-derived species distribution models characterised the migratory corridors and connectivity pathways of mobile marine species across European seas. These analyses directly informed recommendations for ecologically coherent MPA networks that maintain functional connectivity for wide-ranging species across national boundaries.
  <div style="margin-top: 0.75rem; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;"><a href="https://mpa-europe.eu/" target="_blank" class="usecase-read-more"><strong>Read more ›</strong></a></div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> European seas</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> MPA networks designed within national boundaries often fail to protect migratory species that regularly cross jurisdictions, leaving critical corridor habitats unprotected between national systems.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Science-based recommendations for MPA connectivity incorporated into the design of a coherent European MPA network, supporting functional habitat continuity for migratory species.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS occurrence data, species distribution models, regional datasets (European seas)</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Providing the scientific basis for high-seas corridor designation under BBNJ</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-potential">Potential use case</span></div>
  OBIS occurrence data on migratory species in areas beyond national jurisdiction can provide the scientific basis for designating ecologically significant marine areas and connectivity corridors under the BBNJ Agreement's area-based management tools.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> High seas and areas beyond national jurisdiction</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Designating area-based management tools under the BBNJ Agreement requires spatially explicit scientific evidence of ecological significance — yet high-seas biodiversity data is sparse and fragmented across institutions.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Aggregated, globally accessible occurrence data providing the empirical foundation for BBNJ area nominations and high-seas corridor protection.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS API, open-ocean occurrence datasets, migratory species tracking records</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Identifying high-risk overlap zones between migratory megafauna and fishing effort</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-potential">Potential use case</span></div>
  By mapping the predicted presence of migratory megafauna — sea turtles, sharks, marine mammals — against the spatial footprint of fishing effort, OBIS data can identify high-risk overlap zones and inform targeted bycatch reduction measures.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global, with priority in tropical tuna fisheries and highly migratory species corridors</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Bycatch of threatened migratory megafauna is driven by spatiotemporal overlap with fishing fleets that is predictable but not systematically mapped or acted upon.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Spatially explicit bycatch risk maps enabling fisheries authorities to design targeted, evidence-based mitigation measures for high-overlap zones.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS occurrence data, species distribution models, long-term tracking datasets</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Underpinning joint monitoring programmes for species crossing multiple EEZs</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  Standardised, OBIS-compatible occurrence data shared across national nodes can underpin joint monitoring programmes and harmonised management plans for species crossing multiple exclusive economic zones, supporting regional agreements under the Convention on Migratory Species.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global, with priority in regions with active CMS agreements</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Countries managing shared migratory species often lack the interoperable, cross-border data infrastructure needed to coordinate monitoring and demonstrate joint progress under CMS obligations.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Harmonised, nationally contributed occurrence datasets enabling shared monitoring baselines and joint reporting for transboundary migratory species.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> National node datasets, OBIS data standards, cross-node data sharing infrastructure</div>
  </div>
</div>
