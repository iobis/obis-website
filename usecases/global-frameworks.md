<h1><span class="usecase-icon-badge"><i class="bi bi-globe" style="color: var(--tags);"></i></span>Global Frameworks</h1>

OBIS ensures marine biodiversity is represented in global biodiversity platforms, building strong alliances for conservation. By contributing standardised, internationally comparable occurrence data to key international processes and by actively partnering with global biodiversity platforms, OBIS connects the science of marine biodiversity to the policy frameworks that govern it.

*Supports: GBIF–OBIS joint strategy, IOC/UNESCO, CBD, IPBES, SDG 17*

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Providing standardised marine biodiversity data for national CBD reporting</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-implemented">Implemented</span></div>
  OBIS data has been used by countries preparing their national reports to the Convention on Biological Diversity, providing standardised, internationally comparable occurrence records that underpin status assessments and progress evaluations against the targets of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Many countries lack the national marine survey capacity to produce comprehensive, internationally comparable biodiversity status assessments for CBD reporting obligations.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Standardised occurrence data enabling more rigorous and comparable national CBD reports, strengthening marine biodiversity representation within the global policy framework.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS API, national node datasets, long-term occurrence records</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Aligning global biodiversity data infrastructures to eliminate duplication and gaps</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  OBIS and GBIF have developed a joint strategy to align their data infrastructures, eliminate duplication, and ensure comprehensive, consistent marine biodiversity representation within the global open-access biodiversity data landscape — a model for large-scale interoperability between international data platforms.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Parallel biodiversity data infrastructures create duplication, inconsistency, and coverage gaps that undermine the reliability of global biodiversity assessments used by IPBES, CBD, and other frameworks.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> A coherent, interoperable global biodiversity data commons with comprehensive marine coverage, serving as a model for international platform collaboration.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS occurrence records, Darwin Core standards, OBIS–GBIF data sharing agreements</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Integrating OBIS data into broader global biodiversity platforms</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  The TAI4OBIS initiative demonstrates how OBIS data infrastructure can be extended and integrated with complementary systems to strengthen the marine biodiversity data commons, building the partnerships and technical bridges needed to make OBIS data accessible and actionable within broader global biodiversity platforms.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Marine biodiversity data remains siloed from complementary global platforms, limiting its discoverability and use in policy processes that draw on multiple data sources.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Stronger technical bridges between OBIS and global biodiversity platforms, increasing the reach and policy uptake of marine biodiversity data.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS API, occurrence datasets, data interoperability standards</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Strengthening the marine biodiversity evidence base for IPBES science-policy assessments</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-potential">Potential use case</span></div>
  OBIS data can be more systematically integrated into future IPBES global and regional assessments, providing the marine biodiversity empirical foundation for science-policy synthesis reports that inform intergovernmental decision-making on biodiversity loss, ecosystem services, and the effectiveness of conservation responses.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Marine ecosystems are systematically underrepresented in IPBES assessments relative to terrestrial ecosystems, partly due to the difficulty of accessing and harmonising ocean biodiversity data at assessment scales.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> A stronger marine biodiversity evidence base in IPBES assessments, ensuring ocean ecosystems receive commensurate attention in global science-policy processes.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> Global occurrence datasets, long-term time-series records, OBIS API</div>
  </div>
</div>
