<h1><span class="usecase-icon-badge"><i class="bi bi-exclamation-triangle" style="color: var(--tags);"></i></span>Pollution, Habitats &amp; Ecosystem Health</h1>

OBIS helps track habitat degradation, pollution impacts, and ecosystem restoration progress. Its long-term, globally distributed occurrence records provide the historical baselines and monitoring infrastructure needed to detect ecological change, evaluate restoration outcomes, and support evidence-based environmental management.

*Supports: KMGBF Target 7, SDG 14.1, UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration*

<br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Reconstructing historical baselines to measure long-term habitat and community change</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-implemented">Implemented</span></div>
  OBIS multi-decadal occurrence records have been used to reconstruct historical species assemblages and detect shifting baselines — the gradual erosion of ecological benchmarks caused by cumulative human pressures. These analyses reveal long-term trends in habitat quality and community composition that would be invisible in shorter monitoring windows.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Without long-term historical records, managers cannot distinguish natural variability from genuine degradation — leading to management targets anchored to already-impacted states rather than true ecological baselines.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Reconstruction of pre-impact species assemblages and long-term degradation trajectories, enabling more ambitious and ecologically grounded restoration and management targets.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> Long-term occurrence time-series, historical collection records, OBIS API</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Supplying biodiversity baselines for environmental impact assessments of coastal development</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-implemented">Implemented</span></div>
  OBIS occurrence data has been incorporated into environmental impact assessments for coastal and offshore development projects, providing the biodiversity baseline against which potential impacts are evaluated and mitigation measures are designed.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Global</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> EIA processes often rely on short pre-project surveys that miss rare, seasonal, or mobile species — OBIS long-term records can fill these gaps and reveal biodiversity values that brief surveys would overlook.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> More robust EIA baselines reducing the risk of undetected biodiversity impacts from coastal and offshore development.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS API, long-term occurrence data, benthic and habitat records</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Mapping the ecological footprint of coral bleaching events in real time</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  OBIS occurrence data on reef-building corals and associated species can be combined with sea surface temperature anomaly data to map the spatial extent and ecological consequences of bleaching events, supporting real-time response and long-term reef monitoring programmes.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Tropical and subtropical reef systems globally</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Bleaching events unfold faster than in-water survey capacity can track — managers lack the spatial tools to prioritise response efforts or assess cumulative losses across reef systems.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Spatially explicit mapping of bleaching exposure and reef biodiversity supporting triage decisions, emergency response, and long-term reef recovery monitoring.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS coral taxon occurrence records, reef species distribution data, long-term time-series</div>
  </div>
</div>

<br><br><br>

<div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.3;">Measuring seagrass habitat loss and evaluating the success of restoration interventions</div>
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;"><span class="badge rounded-pill status-ongoing">Ongoing</span></div>
  By tracking seagrass species occurrence over time through OBIS, researchers and managers can quantify habitat loss trajectories, identify drivers of decline, and measure the success of restoration interventions — providing the evidence base for adaptive management and international restoration commitments.
  <div style="margin-top: 1.25rem; background-color: var(--light-grey); border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; font-size: 0.9rem;">
    <div><strong>Region:</strong> Coastal zones globally, with priority in temperate and tropical seagrass regions</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Challenge/issue:</strong> Seagrass restoration investments often lack the monitoring infrastructure to demonstrate outcomes — without long-term occurrence data, neither loss trajectories nor recovery signals can be reliably detected.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>Impact:</strong> Evidence-based seagrass loss and recovery assessments supporting adaptive management decisions and the verification of international restoration pledges.</div>
    <div style="margin-top: 1.1rem;"><strong>OBIS inputs:</strong> OBIS seagrass taxon occurrence records, long-term time-series data, regional coastal datasets</div>
  </div>
</div>
