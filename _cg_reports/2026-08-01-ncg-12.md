## Presentation

**OBIS Australia: finding strategies for publishing eDNA data at scale***Presented by Berenice Talamantes Becerra, OBIS Australia (session 1); recapped by Katherine Tattersall (session 2)*OBIS Australia operates under CSIRO and is also involved in running the Marine National Facility, as well as the Australian government-funded research vessel RV Investigator, which has collected marine data for more than 40 years. The same team manages the Node and the data flowing off the vessel, which is increasingly generating eDNA-derived observations that need to be made publicly available and published to OBIS. eDNA data collection is outpacing the publication workflows the Node currently runs and the rate at which people can be upskilled to publish. Across Australian users, sequence files are deposited, but the biodiversity information remains difficult to discover; metadata standards and platforms are fragmented, and the publication step is time-consuming enough that many avoid it. Berenice framed the work of improving the eDNA publication workflow around four components: people, processes, technology, as well as governance and guidelines.The first step of the process consists of a short survey produced by the OBIS Australia team to better understand friction points gathered across five chapters: stakeholders and governance, metadata and standards, technologies and infrastructure, workflow management, and capacity building and communication. The OBIS Nodes are all encouraged to take part in the survey! Although primarily designed for the Australian context, the survey is deliberately being shared more widely so that outcomes can be fed back to the OBIS community and the Secretariat to inform support for Nodes worldwide.Aggregated results and interpretation will be shared with the network; all responses remain anonymous. Deadline to complete: 11 September 2026. **Survey link [here](https://csiro.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_6Ap11Gy6xrDdvCe)****.** (10–15 minutes to complete)

**MBON–OBIS training: from knowledge acquisition to action***Presented by Carolina Peralta, Caribbean OBIS (session 2)*Carolina Peralta presented a training model designed to close the gap between understanding data standards and actually publishing data. Building on the MBON Rocky Shore (pole-to-pole) methodology, the model takes participants through the full research data lifecycle within a single workshop: from planning and standardised field data collection, through data wrangling and formatting, to publishing a concrete dataset into OBIS.This framework has been tested across three events: the ADAPT workshop of the Ocean Best Practices System Capacity Development Working Group, and two MBON–OBIS workshops (one recently completed at the College of Marine Science, University of South Florida; another to be repeated in the Azores in October). The workshops are a collaboration between MBON and the AIR Centre in the Azores, with the ambition of making them an annual activity, yet to be confirmed.**What worked well**Investing time and effort in a tangible final product markedly increases participant motivation and understanding of the value of each step. Participants go beyond learning concepts such as Darwin Core formatting or what OBIS is to produce a dataset that is then actually published in OBIS during the workshop. This creates a strong connection between field observation and the global biodiversity infrastructure.**Reflections for scaling up**The training need not be MBON-specific; the protocol in the workshop was chosen as the simplest way to provide hands-on context. A blended approach could be effective, with online delivery of general concepts, tools and guidelines in advance; and the in-person component focused almost entirely on one-to-one support for data preparation and publication.**The challenges** - Among participants: Uneven computing and Excel skills, uneven level of understanding of data structures (for example, long versus wide formats), and variable coding skills among participants play an important role in how the workshop can unfold. - Fieldwork logistics require major attention. - Integrating AI into these workflows remains an open question for everyone. - Willingness to share data openly can still be a blocker in some regions, which points to the need to build the case for open data and institutional support for it into the trainings themselves.**→ Ruben Perez Perez** asked whether trainees worked on one dataset or several, and about the participants' backgrounds. Carolina Peralta explained that all trainees work on a single dataset collected at rocky shore sites following the standardised method, with three transects and multiple quadrats. Participants split into groups for fieldwork and data cleaning, then merge their subsets into one MBON dataset for that sampling site.

## Lightning talk

**OBIS Poland***Presented by Marcin Wichorowski, Institute of Oceanology, Polish Academy of Sciences (session 1)*OBIS Poland is operated by the [Institute of Oceanology of the Polish Academy of Sciences](https://www.iopan.pl/en/) (IO PAN), by [Marcin Wichorowski](https://oceanexpert.org/expert/16805) (Node Manager) and Monika Berezowska (Data Manager). The Node was established in July 2026, as the natural follow-up to the creation of a National Oceanographic Data Centre in Poland at the end of 2025.IO PAN has a long tradition in marine biodiversity, with core interests in the Baltic Sea and, increasingly over recent years, the Arctic and Antarctic. Flagship activities include long-term ecosystem monitoring in the Svalbard fjords (notably Hornsund and Kongsfjorden), supported by Poland's Arctic station in Hornsund and the long-running AREX research cruises conducted over the past 30 years.Research conducted at IO PAN focuses on tracking biodiversity shifts under a warming Atlantic and melting glaciers, and their effects on marine habitats and carbon cycling, alongside taxonomic inventories of Arctic macrofauna, meiofauna and molluscs. [The nine Polish datasets already present in OBIS](https://obis.org/node/dfef65ad-91ce-4fcb-ae2a-8145f9cbcf0b) cover the Svalbard region, the Baltic Sea, and some records from the Greenland Sea.Biodiversity data work at IO PAN traces back to 1996 through collaboration with the Norwegian Polar Institute and successive European projects (BIODAF, MARBEF and, currently, MARBEFES, a Horizon Europe project involving the Flanders Marine Institute among other institutions).Public outreach and citizen science are part of the Node's mission, including a BioBlitz organised in Sopot in 2023.**→ Ruben Perez Perez** asked which platform was used to run the BioBlitz, noting that iNaturalist can push research-grade records to GBIF automatically. Marcin responded that no external platform such as iNaturalist or observation.org was used; the full process (planning, preparation, sample acquisition and lab work) was supervised by marine biologists from IO PAN, with results published on the MARBEFES website.

## Open discussion: NCG succession and SG-OBIS-14

**NCG Co-Chair succession**Both John Nicholls and Maria Cornthwaite are stepping down as NCG Co-Chairs after SG-OBIS-14. New Co-Chairs are needed, and all Nodes are warmly encouraged to volunteer or to nominate a colleague. The Co-Chairs stressed that the commitment is very achievable even for Nodes with low staffing and that the Secretariat provides strong support. The NCG Co-Chair role also brings a seat on the OBIS Executive Committee and a genuine say in the direction of OBIS. John and Maria will remain available to support incoming Co-Chairs through a potential transition.→ **Martha Vides** suggested the forthcoming Node survey include a friendly, direct question on willingness to Co-Chair, capturing both interest and the reasons people might decline.

**SG-OBIS-14 logistics**The Steering Group meeting will be held face-to-face in Oostende, Belgium, 11–16 November 2026, back-to-back with the World Conference on Marine Biodiversity in Bruges to make the trip worthwhile for those attending both. This is an unusual year: because the cadence of IODE and IOC meetings has shifted, the work plan and budget must be finalised beforehand through two online pre-Steering Group meetings (weeks commencing 14 and 21 September).→ **Ward Appeltans** asked members to confirm participation as soon as possible. On logistics, lunches are expected to be covered, and the Secretariat is exploring shared, cost-effective accommodation options for interested Nodes. Members were encouraged to send the Secretariat any questions they need answered for their host organisations. **In-person versus online participation.**Responding to Carolina Peralta and Martha Vides on behalf of OBIS LAC, Katherine Tattersall explained that the Steering Group meeting has traditionally been face-to-face, with occasional limited online participation (an audio link, or chat-only interventions) rather than a full hybrid experience, given the technical overhead and cost. The strong preference remains for in-person attendance, as remote participation captures only a fraction of the benefit. Nodes facing a travel-cost barrier were urged to contact the Secretariat before ruling out attendance so that that support can be explored; where the obstacle. is a scheduling conflict, the Secretariat will do its best to facilitate input. Echoing a point raised in session 1, Nodes were reminded that inclusivity is a core OBIS strength and that no Node should be left behind for lack of resources.

The implementation plan for developing metrics to quantify and monitor EOV data within OBIS is complete. The team has moved to a proof of concept that uses AI to extract Darwin Core terms from GOOS EOV specification sheets (starting with seagrass), transform them into LinkML, and build logic to identify EOV datasets among OBIS Darwin Core records. Work now focuses on refining that logic across the many ways a dataset can qualify; the intention is to continue and automate this in next year’s work plan.eDNA training. An online eDNA workshop was held in July (recording available), and an in-person course ran at INVEMAR (OBIS Colombia) with participants from the LAC group; slides from both are on the OBIS training page. The eDNA subgroup has developed this tool with support from Steve Formel as a modular, Carpentries-style eDNA data management course intended as a template for future OBIS courses.Taxon annotations. A team drawn mainly from OBIS UK, in collaboration with WoRMS, will be trained in mid-September to help validate automated taxon annotations; the implementation plan is complete, with roughly 20 volunteers lined up.
