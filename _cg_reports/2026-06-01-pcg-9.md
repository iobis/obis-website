## Introduction

The OBIS Products Coordination Group met in two sessions. The second session was run by Silas Principe, as Co-Chairs Stephen Formel and Jon Pye were travelling. The agenda covered design updates to the Products Catalogue, the draft catalogue governance, and a review of what the group had heard over the past six months, to focus its work on Outcome 8 for the next six months.

## Products Catalogue

**Design updates.** Silas Principe presented updates to the catalogue in the development instance, aimed at making the pages more attractive and contemporary. Highlighted projects are now more visible. Product types follow the Zenodo/DataCite vocabulary, while thematic areas are curated by OBIS and will be updated to align with the OBIS website. Stephen Formel added that work continues on publishing OBIS GitHub repositories so that they can serve as good examples for others to follow.

→ Ward Appeltans asked whether relationships between products are being created. Stephen Formel confirmed that a clear mechanism exists, but this has not yet been a priority. Ward Appeltans also asked whether presentations could be linked to products.

**Alternative hosts.** In response to a question about hosts other than Zenodo, it was explained that products can be entered manually, but would then need to be kept up to date by hand, whereas with Zenodo the catalogue follows Zenodo's versioning. GitHub can be linked to Zenodo, so that a new release triggers an update in Zenodo and in the catalogue.

→ Katherine Tattersall noted that the Zenodo route supports all Nodes, including those without their own metadata catalogues. For a Node with a mature catalogue and established harvest and push pathways, such as the Australian Node, it would be preferable to host products in its own catalogue and work with the Secretariat on pathways for harvesting directly, provided this is useful to a critical number of other Nodes; a needs assessment may be required. She also asked whether the metadata harvesting protocols used by ODIS, another IOC IODE programme, had been considered for harvesting across any number of registered repositories, identifying OBIS products through appropriate metadata elements.

**Draft governance.** Stephen Formel presented the draft governance, which will guide the quality assurance that admits products to the catalogue, with the goal of keeping the catalogue at a quality that satisfies the OBIS network. Members were asked to comment by 15 June, so that feedback could be presented at the OBIS Executive Committee meeting.

→ Ward Appeltans asked what the criteria for inclusion are, and pointed out a mismatch in product types between the governance document and the catalogue proposal, for example in how presentations, datasets and derived datasets are treated. Silas Principe explained that the catalogue currently uses the DataCite vocabulary, which can be changed.

## Outcome 8: data product pilot

Stephen Formel presented what the group had heard and learned over the previous six months, as the basis for focusing on Outcome 8: a data product pilot showcasing OBIS' ability to serve national policy outcomes and highlighting the richness of data available through OBIS national and regional Nodes. Background is available in the Steering Group report (https://oceanexpert.org/document/37512).

**Who to consult, and on what.** Laurent Chmiel asked whether the discussion should be opened to a larger group, giving Nodes time to consult their governments on what they need. Silas Principe asked whether there had been a survey of products developed by Nodes, and noted that the information required by countries varies a great deal. Katherine Tattersall identified two kinds of data that member states can use: products built from aggregated data, for example based on the MPA Europe work, and statistics on data access and usage. Ward Appeltans noted that an ongoing survey is collecting information on Nodes' stakeholders, which could inform useful products. Laurent Chmiel observed that this survey concerns funders rather than users, and that a separate survey could target users.

**The CBD as a starting point.** Dan Lear stressed that the pilot is a proof of concept and should be something with the most external impact, with global policy relevance. The CBD is the obvious choice: OBIS is listed among the complementary indicators for Targets 20 and 21 of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. He asked whether the pilot could be opened up beyond the OBIS community. Stephen Formel agreed, while cautioning about time, and also saw CBD reporting as the obvious starting point. Victor Chocho said he was interested in meeting the minimal reporting requirement for Target 21, while noting that other countries may have more complex requirements.

→ Stephen Formel asked how relevant CBD reporting is to thematic Nodes. Jon Pye said it does not matter to OTN directly, but expected it to matter to OBIS Canada, and noted that CIOOS has started a project to help bring OTN's Canadian datasets into the national picture. Individual projects have also asked OTN for help preparing packages for initiatives such as ISRA, which could be of interest as general products. On whether Nodes benefit from being part of the funding, he noted that OTN is funded to be infrastructure, which often allows it to do things without needing money to come back.

→ Ben Donnelly explained that OBIS-SEAMAP provides information to other entities for CBD and EBSA reporting, and that it would do no harm for them to be able to obtain it themselves. OBIS-SEAMAP produces catalogue and data reports linked to specific meetings, where a snapshot of the state of OBIS data at the time matters: for example, species richness in the Eastern Tropical Pacific at the time of the workshop where the Salas y Gómez and Nazca Ridges were proposed as an ecologically important area (https://chm.cbd.int/en/database/record?documentID=204100). The Duke lab's modelling work supports the Marine Mammal Protection Act, and in the US it is modelling, rather than unsynthesized observation data, that supports much decision-making, so it would be valuable to bring it into the catalogue. He shared the OBIS-SEAMAP model site (https://seamap.env.duke.edu/models/), an example of a model's release history that shows which version was used at a given date or decision process (https://seamap.env.duke.edu/models/Duke/EC/EC_Atlantic_spotted_dolphin_history.html), and the mapper for that model's data (https://seamap.env.duke.edu/models/mapper/EC?species=Atlantic%20spotted%20dolphin). How ready the SEAMAP models are to be added to products.obis.org under the draft governance guidelines will need research and assessment by the Duke lab.

→ Victor Chocho pointed out that many Nodes are not part of government, so the Secretariat could make it easy for a national Node to pull the information with the click of a button, as GBIF does, though some countries may need more. Another target concerns the number of publications (https://ort.cbd.int/es/national-reports/nr7).

**A simple first step.** Stephen Formel proposed focusing on implementing something very simple, such as a button that meets Ecuador's needs, with the main deliverable for October being a follow-up discussion on how to build on it for the more nuanced needs of the Nodes. There was general agreement, with the caveat that this may depend on the Secretariat working with Victor Chocho. How the outcome will be presented to the NCG remains to be discussed.
