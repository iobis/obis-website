## OBIS Multilingual Strategy

Recognizing the limitations of currently offering OBIS content exclusively in English, the OBIS Secretariat has initiated the development of a multilingual content strategy to broaden reach and improve community engagement. The full document is accessible here ([https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1nNOBYOVi5OyVOUCD9Bsjntr2XWp74Eac6G9Ghweb8GM/edit?usp=sharing](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1nNOBYOVi5OyVOUCD9Bsjntr2XWp74Eac6G9Ghweb8GM/edit?usp=sharing)). The strategy involves:

→ Translating the OBIS website and official documents into additional languages, starting with Spanish.

→ Utilizing DeepL for machine translation, followed by community-based human proofreading to ensure quality and accuracy.

→  a distributed workflow, with designated groups of translators and coordinators, to prevent overburdening any single individual and to build a critical mass of contributors.

This effort will be rolled out in close collaboration with the OBIS Slack community, and further details will be shared as the initiative progresses. The Secretariat emphasized that this is a community-driven effort and extended a call to action for Spanish-speaking Nodes and contributors to participate.

## Flash Talks

### MedOBIS: Regional Contributions and Data Stewardship

Presented by Melina Loulakaki (MedOBIS, HCMR, Greece)

Melina Loulakaki introduced the work and recent activities of MedOBIS, the Mediterranean Node of OBIS, hosted by the Institute of Marine Biology, Biotechnology and Aquaculture at the Hellenic Centre for Marine Research (HCMR) in Greece. MedOBIS provides access to marine biodiversity data spanning a wide temporal range—from 1841 to 2024—and covering a variety of sources. The Node collaborates with numerous countries across the Mediterranean region, embracing the area's linguistic, cultural, and political diversity, and contributing to global efforts in FAIR and open data. The MedOBIS team consists of four core members: Dimitris Mpabrakis (Environmental Scientist), Ioannis Israilidis (Environmental Biologist),

Melina Loulakaki (Computer Scientist), and external collaborator Dr. Vassilios Vassiliou, Assistant Professor at Ionian University.

Recent data management efforts have focused on two workflows:

For new datasets: collection, formatting, quality control, and publishing.

For historical datasets: digitization followed by the same quality-controlled publication pipeline.

The Node ensures feedback loops with data providers and is committed to the long-term preservation of datasets and metadata to guarantee future interpretability. Recent achievements include:

Training and outreach in data-deficient Mediterranean countries

Internal mobilization to encourage data sharing among local researchers.

Exploration of historical data through public science platforms like Zooniverse.

A new MedOBIS website is also set to launch soon, improving visibility and access.

OBIS-SEAMAP Metadata and DOI Integration  
Presented by Ei Fujioka (Marine Geospatial Ecology Lab, Duke University)

Ei Fujioka introduced recent developments for OBIS-SEAMAP, the OBIS product managed and developed at Duke University’s Marine Geospatial Ecology Lab (MGEL). The focus of the update was on improving metadata sharing and discoverability through integration with the DataONE repository network. Ei’s key updates included:

OBIS-SEAMAP is now preparing to share dataset metadata with DataONE, a more

generalized metadata aggregator. This move supports collaboration with the Regional Wildlife

Science Collaborative (RWSC), which is using DataONE as a core metadata source for offshore

wind research in the U.S. Atlantic.

To align with DataONE standards, OBIS-SEAMAP will mint Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) for its datasets, likely through DataCite via Duke University Libraries.

Metadata will be exposed in schema.org JSON-LD format and indexed using sitemap.xml, both of which enhance discoverability via search engines like Google and Bing.

This process allows DataONE to automatically harvest and build a dedicated OBIS-SEAMAP Node on its platform. Once DOIs are in place and metadata updated, the Node will go live. A noted limitation of schema.org metadata is its lack of specificity for marine biodiversity, such as no direct field for species coverage. OBIS-SEAMAP is currently listing species names as keywords to address this gap. The prototype of the OBIS-SEAMAP Node on DataONE was briefly demonstrated, and Fujioka concluded by emphasizing that this effort will improve both machine discoverability and global integration of OBIS-SEAMAP datasets.

## Guided discussion

Ruben Perez (EurOBIS) proposed the following topics to discussion:

### 1. OBIS Network Procedure

Ruben Perez Perez (EurOBIS) presented a GitHub-based workflow developed to streamline the triage and endorsement of marine datasets published through GBIF. This system is centered around the OBIS Network GitHub repository, which automatically generates issues when datasets tagged with the “OBIS network” identifier are published in GBIF.

Key Features:

A GitHub Action monitors GBIF for new marine datasets and creates issues in the repository.

Node managers subscribed to the repository are notified when relevant datasets appear.

Nodes can:

Assign datasets to themselves by applying their Node label

Endorse datasets by adding the endorsed label, signalling readiness for OBIS harvesting.

This approach reduces manual communication with the OBIS Secretariat and supports decentralized quality control. But some Nodes were unaware of the system or its intended use, prompting a call for better onboarding. Pieter Provoost (OBIS Secretariat) asked whether the Secretariat should pre-assign datasets or whether Nodes prefer to self-assign. Responses varied. Ruben noted that overlapping regional scopes make automatic assignment complicated.

Quality Assurance Role: Nodes raised questions about expectations regarding data quality. Ruben clarified that Nodes should assess datasets before endorsement and may contact GBIF publishers to improve problematic data.

Criteria for Marine Datasets: Yi-Ming Gan (OBIS Antarctica) asked for clarity on what qualifies as a “marine dataset.” Pieter and Ruben agreed that even datasets with a small percentage of marine records are of interest to OBIS, but stressed the need to better document inclusion criteria to avoid confusion and misinterpretation.

Next Steps: Stephen Formel (OBIS Secretariat) suggested that the discussion continue in the GitHub issue threads, where workflow improvements can be collaboratively developed, with an additional Data Coordination Group meeting being called to collate the issues and ensure discussions are captured within the OBIS framework.

### 2. OBIS Node Pages

Ruben initiated a second discussion on the purpose and logic behind OBIS Node pages on the OBIS website, which display datasets linked to each Node.

Central Questions Raised:

Should Node pages display only endorsed datasets?

Can a dataset appear under multiple Nodes?

Should Node pages reflect contributions, coordination, or both?

Ruben used examples such as EurOBIS hosting Norwegian data prior to the creation of the OBIS Norway Node. Should those datasets now be shown under Norway, or under both Nodes? Yi-Ming Gan (OBIS Antarctica) proposed a funding-based approach: datasets should be linked to the Node of the funding country. Stephen Formel (OBIS Secretariat) advocated for flexibility: Node pages should support multiple levels of aggregation—national, thematic (e.g., Antarctic), or regional—depending on the context and audience. Ruben emphasized the importance of defining the purpose of Node pages: are they primarily for recognizing Node contributions, or should they serve as entry points for data discovery?

### 3. OBIS–GBIF Nodes Cooperation

Ruben’s third topic focused on improving collaboration between OBIS Nodes and GBIF Nodes, especially in cases of overlapping mandates or shared geographic focus.

Context:  
Following recent conversations at the GBIF Europe and Central Asia Nodes Meeting, Ruben highlighted the need for better coordination to avoid duplicated efforts and ensure mutual recognition of contributions.

Specific Concerns:  
→ When multiple OBIS Nodes have overlapping geographic scopes (e.g., OBIS MedOBIS and EurOBIS in the Mediterranean), who should process and endorse shared datasets?

→ Can multiple OBIS Nodes appear on the same dataset without requiring double endorsement?

→ How can OBIS avoid conflicting or redundant data flows when GBIF Nodes publish marine data independently?

Ruben suggested that tagging multiple Nodes in GitHub might help with initial triage, but acknowledged this still requires community input to decide endorsement and attribution. The discussion highlighted the need for:

→ Better documentation of triage responsibilities between OBIS and GBIF Nodes.

→ Clarified rules for shared dataset visibility across OBIS Node pages.

→ Continued dialogue to refine the endorsement process and strengthen cross-infrastructure cooperation.
