## Introduction

The OBIS Products Coordination Group met in two sessions. The agenda covered the status of the Products Catalogue and a planned presentation from IMOS and OBIS-AU on aggregating Australian seabird data. The presentation was not given in either session and was carried over to a later meeting.

## Status of the Products Catalogue

**A static alternative to CKAN.** Stephen Formel proposed an alternative to CKAN, prompted by continued difficulty getting CKAN working again after each customization. The idea is a static website built on GitHub Pages, where each product is published as a static web page rather than as a dynamic page populated from a backend database. A prototype is available at https://iobis.github.io/obis-products-catalog-eleventy/. Users would create and update entries through GitHub permissions, pull requests and a YAML registry. The trade-offs are that the static version has no API and no geospatial search.

**Continued CKAN development.** Work on the CKAN instance continues, with the aim of building connectivity to ODIS and the discoverability of the datasets referenced there.

**A broader consideration.** Some repositories, such as FigShare, are now blocking schema.org scraping in order to block AI crawlers, and are forcing access through their APIs instead. This affects how a catalogue can harvest product metadata.

## Licensing and AI

The meeting closed on a discussion of how AI use bears on data licensing, prompted by Creative Commons' work on CC signals (https://creativecommons.org/ai-and-the-commons/cc-signals/). This is primarily a matter for the data side, but products will also have to reckon with it. Stephen Formel noted that initiatives such as FAIR for AI and AI Ready are considering these questions. The group discussed the ethical dimension: open data rests on the assumption that a licence can be depended on, and AI use may undermine that assumption. Once licences that explicitly address AI use are available, data providers might be asked to revisit the licences they have issued so that they are explicit on this point. Katherine Tattersall shared an example of data being passed through AI tools without attribution or consideration, and urged the community to be clear and visible about the expectations it sets as it learns more.
