The inaugural OBIS Nodes Coordination Group (NCG) meeting took place on November 21st and 22nd, 2024. To ensure inclusivity across time zones, the NCG administration organized identical online sessions: the first on November 21st at 15:00 UTC and the second on November 22nd at 7:00 UTC. Together, these sessions welcomed 36 participants, fostering engaging and productive discussions that set the foundation for future collaboration within the OBIS community.

The NCG meetings aim to create a platform of dialogue between Nodes to share experiences and best practices, find solutions to common issues, celebrate achievements, and collaborate in all possible forms.

The NCG is co-chaired by Maria Cornthwaite (OBIS Canada ([https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/data-donnees/obis/index-eng.html](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/science/data-donnees/obis/index-eng.html))) and John Nicholls (Oceans Past Initiative ([https://oceanspast.org/](https://oceanspast.org/))), who bring their leadership, expertise and collaborative spirit to guide this collective effort. The secretariat of the NCG meetings is supported by Laurent Chmiel (OBIS secretariat).

This report consolidates both sessions' insights, questions, comments, and interviews into a clear and cohesive format.

Participants: Yong-Rock An (OBIS Korea), Ward Appeltans (OBIS Secretariat), SangHo Baek (OBIS Korea), Nicolas Bailly (Fish OBIS), Laurent Chmiel (OBIS Secretariat), Victor Chocho (OBIS Ecuador), Maria Cornthwaite (OBIS Canada), Angela Dini (OTN-OBIS), Kit Elloran (SEA OBIS), Ahmad Fakhrurrazi (OBIS Malaysia), Chloe Figueroa Ashforth (OBIS UK), Stephen Formel (OBIS USA), Yi-Ming Gan (Antarctic OBIS), Luciana Genio (OBIS ISA), Hólmgrímur Helgasson (Arctic OBIS), Johnny Konjarla (IndOBIS), Elizabeth Lawrence (OBIS Secretariat), Dan Lear (OBIS UK), Kevin Mackay (SWP OBIS), Dimitra Mavraki (MedOBIS), Fatuma Mzingirwa (OBIS Kenya), John Nicholls (Oceans Past Initiative), Carolina Peralta (CaribbeanOBIS), Silas Principe (OBIS Secretariat), Anton Van de Putte (Antarctic OBIS), Jon Pye (OTN-OBIS), Sachit Rajbhandari (OBIS-AU), Tshikana Rasehlomi (AfrOBIS), Andres Roubicek (OBIS Australia), Katherine Tattersall (OBIS Australia), Leen Vandepitte (EurOBIS).

## 2- Updates from the OBIS Steering group, presented by Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall (OBIS SG co-chairs)

Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall expressed their excitement to take part in this inaugural NCG meeting. They reminded the participants that the format of the meeting is experimental and open to feedback from the community. Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall then reported on the sixth session of the OBIS Executive Committee (OBIS EC-6) in Oostende from October 14 to 16, 2024, highlighting the objectives of the meeting: reinforcing OBIS cross-community coordination, strengthening and improving communications and crafting a long-term vision for the organization. The Co-Chairs reminded the participants the full report can be accessed on Ocean Expert [https://oceanexpert.org/document/35296](https://oceanexpert.org/document/35296)

The OBIS Executive Committee has established three topic-centered Coordination Groups to address the need for reinforced coordination. The Product Coordination Group (PCG) focuses on creating high-quality, science-based, user-friendly data products, including biodiversity indicators and maps. These products aim to be robust, actionable, and trustworthy. The Data Coordination Group (DCG) is dedicated to ensuring the quality and FAIRness (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) of OBIS data by implementing quality-control processes, supporting Nodes in data publishing, and resolving technical challenges in data management. Meanwhile, the Nodes Coordination Group (NCG) is charged with fostering collaboration and capacity-building across the network, offering training, mentoring, and regular engagement to ensure Nodes can contribute effectively. The Coordination Groups will meet regularly and maintain an ongoing dialogue with the OBIS community. To facilitate exchanges and enhance support, they plan to develop tools and platforms such as forums and wikis, enabling contributors to collectively address challenges, share experiences, and strengthen their efforts within the network.

Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall reminded the NCG that during the 6th Executive Committee meeting, participants emphasized the need to reinforce OBIS as a brand to solidify its recognition as a global leader in marine biodiversity data. While OBIS is widely acknowledged as a robust, independent, and trustworthy data source, the committee noted that its scientific contributions and societal impact could be showcased more effectively. To address this, the committee identified four development paths:

OBIS's perception should be strengthened through coherent and unified actions across its network.

The OBIS website interface must be improved to better meet diverse user needs and align with modern design standards. Impactful narratives should be produced to highlight OBIS's scientific excellence and real-world impact in marine science and conservation.

A set of strategic messages should be crafted to position OBIS as a leading global scientific infrastructure. These actions will be expanded and integrated into a broader, long-term communication and branding strategy aimed at elevating OBIS's visibility and influence.

## 3- Updates from the OBIS Secretariat, presented by Ward Appeltans (OBIS Programme Manager)

Ward Appeltans, OBIS Programme Manager, delivered a presentation on the significant recognition received by OBIS during the 16th Conference of the Parties (COP16) to the Convention on Biological Diversity. He emphasized that COP16 marked a pivotal moment for ocean observations and marine conservation, highlighting the critical roles that OBIS, GOOS, and ODIS play in advancing global biodiversity objectives.

Ward Appeltans highlighted that during COP16, OBIS was included in the GBF indicator framework as a complementary indicator for [Goal D](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/goals) (Invest and Collaborate), [Target 20](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets/20) (Strengthen Capacity-Building, Technology Transfer, and Scientific and Technical Cooperation for Biodiversity), and [Target 21](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets/21) (Ensure That Knowledge Is Available and Accessible To Guide Biodiversity Action). Ward Appeltans also explained that the Parties adopted the second cycle of the Ecologically or Biologically Significant Marine Areas (EBSA), reminding the meeting participants that OBIS provided vital information on marine species distributions, habitats, and ecosystems, contributing to identifying and describing 321 EBSAs across the ocean during the first EBSA cycle.

Ward Appeltans reported on numerous contacts between OBIS and the World Bank, with a workshop in preparation to create a joint initiative to make marine observations more accessible and actionable. Ward Appeltans also reported that the World Bank is trying to integrate a GBIF-conformity mechanism into its contracts, along with a World Bank-funded helpdesk. A similar mechanism completed with a helpdesk could be set in place with OBIS in the coming years, leveraging the strategic importance of our infrastructure.

Ward Appeltans shared several additional updates with the NCG, reflecting recent milestones and ongoing initiatives within the OBIS network. He highlighted the launch of a new OTGA/OBIS self-paced course titled "Fundamentos para la publicación de datos de biodiversidad marina en OBIS ([https://classroom.oceanteacher.org/mod/forum/discuss.php?d=4993](https://classroom.oceanteacher.org/mod/forum/discuss.php?d=4993))," designed to enhance capacity among Spanish-speaking participants. Available until January 30, 2025, the course focuses on marine biodiversity data standards, quality control, and publishing processes. This initiative underscores OBIS's commitment to building capacity and expanding its reach across diverse linguistic and geographic communities. In addition, Ward Appeltans shared OBIS's involvement in the "Hack for a Livable Planet" hackathon. This event leverages publicly available biodiversity data to develop actionable tools to address critical biodiversity challenges.

Ward Appeltans also announced the recent expansion of the OBIS network with the addition of GBIF Ecuador as an official Node in October 2024. He noted that GBIF Norway is set to join the network in the coming months, further strengthening the global representation and collaboration within OBIS. These new Nodes reflect OBIS's growing influence and the increasing recognition of its value in marine biodiversity data management.

He concluded by emphasizing the importance of these milestones in enhancing OBIS's capacity to support the global community, improve biodiversity data sharing, and foster meaningful collaboration between regional and thematic Nodes. These developments reinforce OBIS's vital contribution to global biodiversity observation and conservation efforts.

The NCG Co-Chairs thanked Ward Appeltans for his report and gave the floor to the Data Coordination Group interim Co-Chairs, Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall.

## 4- Updates from the OBIS Data Coordination Group

Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall provided a detailed update on the Data Coordination Group (DCG) and its strategic priorities. They started their presentation by reminding the meeting participants that the DCG seeks permanent co-chairs. The interim Co-Chairs welcomed and encouraged applications from the NCG members. Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall began by highlighting the adoption of a work plan during the OBIS EC-6 meeting, which sets a roadmap for achieving key goals. The DCG will convene five regular meetings through October 2025, working towards establishing a Steering Group-approved data work plan for 2026-2027.

The co-chairs then outlined six critical action points for 2025. These include delivering implementation guidelines to OBIS Nodes for at least 90% of existing biodiversity Essential Ocean Variables (EOV) specifications, conducting a survey to evaluate Node engagement in external data-related groups, compiling a Register of Engagement for all OBIS Nodes by the end of 2025, ensuring functional integration of OBIS data with the IOC data architecture by the end of 2025, completing a review of the eDNA data extension for Darwin Core (DwC) and the eDNA FAIR checklist complete with a final report with actionable recommendations to the Joint GBIF/OBIS DNA Derived Data Guidelines Working Group and, lastly, assessing Nodes' ability to provide long-term data archiving, presenting a tiered, CoreTrustSeal-aligned model to the NCG by the end of 2025. Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall emphasized the importance of these initiatives in ensuring high-quality, interoperable, and accessible data across the OBIS network. The DCG Co-Chairs recognized the high ambitions of this plan and expressed their confidence that the group will achieve its goals by the end of 2025.

The NCG Co-Chairs thanked Dan Lear and Katherine Tattersall for their report, reminded the participants that the DCG is still seeking permanent chairs, and encouraged anyone interested in the role to come forward. They then transitioned the discussion by giving the floor to the Product Coordination Group Co-Chairs, Jon Pye and Stephen Formel.

## 5- Updates from the OBIS Product Coordination Group

Jon Pye and Stephen Formel, co-chairs of the Product Coordination Group (PCG), provided an update on the group's progress and future plans, reflecting the work plan adopted during the OBIS Executive Committee's Sixth Session (EC-6). They highlighted that the PCG will hold five regular meetings through October 2025 to monitor progress and ensure alignment with the overarching goals of OBIS. These meetings aim to establish a Steering Group-approved data work plan for 2026-2027, setting a long-term direction for developing and delivering OBIS products. The PCG Co-Chairs reminded that the group is warmly welcoming participants and encouraged the audience members to join. The first PCG meeting will take place in December 2024: a Doodle link has been sent to determine the most suitable date.

The Co-Chairs detailed four key action points that the PCG is committed to achieving by the end of 2025, including a comprehensive plan to guide the development of an "OBIS Products Catalogue," outlining its scope, functionality, and user needs; the set-up of a developmental version of the catalogue for testing, allowing for feedback and iterative improvements; the creation of a developmental "JupyterHub" environment, providing enhanced tools for data analysis and visualization; Finally, the production and publication of a finalized "OBIS Products Catalogue," ensuring its accessibility and alignment with the needs of the OBIS community.

Jon Pye and Stephen Formel emphasized that these initiatives will play a crucial role in improving the usability and impact of OBIS products. By delivering high-quality, user-friendly tools and resources, the PCG aims to strengthen OBIS's contributions to global marine biodiversity data accessibility and analysis. They concluded by encouraging active participation and feedback from the Nodes to support the successful implementation of these goals.

The NCG Co-Chairs thanked Jon Pye and Stephen Formel for their report and opened the Guided Discussion. They reminded participants that this portion of the meeting is intended to provide a platform for sharing experiences, addressing challenges, and collaboratively finding solutions that benefit all Nodes. The NCG Co-Chairs then introduced the Flash Talks.

## 6- Flash Talks

The Co-Chairs introduced the concept of flash talks: brief presentations, limited to 3 minutes, where a Node showcases itself to the other nodes - for example, by highlighting its members, activities, challenges, and/or recent achievements. Each flash talk is followed by a Q&A session, fostering interaction and knowledge sharing. Two flash talks are scheduled per bi-monthly meeting, with the same talks presented in each meeting session. Several weeks before each meeting, the NCG co-chairs and secretariat will use a virtual dice roll to select nodes to invite to present flash talks.

## 7- Guided Discussion

The NCG Co-Chairs encouraged participants to actively engage by raising topics of interest and contributing to the dialogue. The Co-Chairs offered participants the opportunity to engage by speaking during the meeting or offering questions and discussion points in the meeting chat, or by contacting the co-chairs and NCG secretariat by email or through the OBIS Slack channel after the meeting.

Ward Appeltans remarked that the mention of OBIS in the Convention on Biological Diversity's Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (CBD GBF) indicator framework could help Nodes get more resources from the hosting country and invited Nodes managers to explore that path. The new IOC Executive Secretary, Mr Vidar Helgesen, has expressed great support for OBIS, which could greatly aid in advancing this endeavour. If needed, at Node request, a high-level meeting can be organised between Mr Vidar Helgesen and the Node hosting country’s government representatives to raise the national Nodes profile, which could help in fundraising nationally.

Victor Chocho highlighted the GBIF Biodiversity Information for Development (BID) program for the Latin America and Caribbean (LAC) region, emphasizing its potential to support biodiversity data initiatives. Katherine Tattersall shared her experience from the GBIF BID Pacific meeting, where she collaborated with the GBIF Secretariat to explore how OBIS could help address biodiversity data gaps in the Oceania region. She noted that such engagements provide valuable insights into strengthening regional data contributions and partnerships. Victor confirmed his role as the regional representative for LAC and discussed ongoing efforts to enhance collaboration between OBIS and GBIF to maximize the impact of biodiversity data initiatives in the region. Stephen Formel inquired about the current number of joint GBIF-OBIS Nodes, identifying SCAR, USA, Ecuador, and Norway as active participants. Victor Chocho added that Brazil is in the process of becoming a joint GBIF-OBIS Node, further expanding the collaboration network.

Victor Chocho highlighted opportunities to enhance the workflow for endorsing datasets from the GitHub repository. He emphasized the potential to improve clarity in task management and role assignments, ensuring a more streamlined and transparent process. He also pointed out that addressing the handling of closed issues would help maintain continuity and provide greater flexibility for contributors to fulfill their responsibilities effectively.

Tshikana Rasehlomi (AfrOBIS) explained that AfrOBIS is in the process of digitizing collected marine biodiversity observations dating back to the 1930s. AfrOBIS is developing a strategy and processes to rescue data, with dedicated software and hardware equipment to rescue various data in various stages, including hard-to-decipher handwritten data. He asked for guidance on the issue and inquired about the community's experience treating ancient data. Tshikana Rasehlomi specified that the collected data will be integrated into OBIS.

John Nicholls, the NCG Co-Chair, replied to Tshikana Rasehlomi by indicating that the Node he manages, the Oceans Past Initiative, actively rescues ancient data, including handwritten documents. John Nicholls pledged to engage further on the topic. He reminded the meeting participants that we need the past to understand the present.

Katherine Tattersall stated that collective effort on the topic is crucial. She explained that an intern starting at CSIRO will look into data rescue, old records and handwritten material and is open to collecting best practices and published papers for guidance.

Maria Cornthwaite disclosed she has a colleague working on technological aids for data rescue, such as scanning methods to acquire material in various conditions. who is currently developing best practices on the topic. Maria will connect with that colleague.

Dimitra Mavraki (MedOBIS) expressed the significance of historical datasets and the importance of digitizing them and integrating them into OBIS.

Ward Appeltans reminded the meeting’s participants that good practices and a leading position for OBIS on the topic could help attract funding.

Yi-Ming Gan (ANTARCTIC OBIS) mentioned the SPNHC group and their work highlighting the challenges of digitalizing past data. SPNHC is a community that should be engaged in historical data. Abstracts on the topic from the group are available at [https://spnhc.org/get-involved/emerging-professionals/](https://spnhc.org/get-involved/emerging-professionals/)

A dedicated "data rescue" public Slack channel was created here ([https://obishq.slack.com/archives/C082DRENRCZ](https://obishq.slack.com/archives/C082DRENRCZ)) to support further discussion.

Ward Appeltans highlighted the availability of the OBIS Nodes training course on OTGA, emphasizing that all Nodes should participate. He noted that while the course requires a commitment, it ensures participants are up to date on all relevant topics. Ward Appeltans also noted the inclusion of a module on eDNA but noted that the training currently lacks content on the new inclusion of occurrence ID in the DNA derived data extension. He suggested that this gap be addressed, and the Data Coordination Group (DCG) could take the discussion further.
