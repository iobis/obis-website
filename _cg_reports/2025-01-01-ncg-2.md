## Introduction

John Nicholls and Maria Cornthwaite, the OBIS Nodes Coordination Group co-chairs, opened the meeting by welcoming all participants and emphasized the importance of the OBIS Nodes Coordination Group meetings to reinforce cohesion among Nodes, improve coordination, and increase collaboration. ## Updates from the OBIS Steering Group

Katherine Tattersall provided updates on two upcoming international conferences where OBIS has a strategic presence. She detailed the [3rd International Ocean Data Conference (IODC-III)](https://oceandataconference.org/), which will take place in Colombia on 10-11 March 2025 and online, reminding the extended abstract submission deadline to January 31st to encourage OBIS nodes to submit abstracts. The first session of the conference, entitled "Ocean Biodiversity (and how we can collectively contribute to the needs of BBNJ and GBF)", will be co-chaired by Katherine and Dan Lear. She also reminded that IODC-III will be running alongside the [28th Session of the IODE Committee](https://oceanexpert.org/event/4258), from 12th to 14th of March.

Katherine Tattersall also introduced the [Living Data 2025](https://livingdata2025.com/) conference scheduled for 21-24 October 2025 in Bogotá, which will also serve as the OBIS All Hands Meeting. Several other OBIS-related meetings will be taking place before the Living Data 2025 conference (see below "Updates from the Secretariat"). Organized with TDWG, GBIF, OBIS, and GEOBON, Living Data 2025 is organised around four themes: (1) Open Data, (2) Data Integration, (3) Biodiversity Data Application and (4) Community Engagement and Capacity Building. Katherine urged OBIS Nodes to submit session proposals and abstracts to ensure OBIS representation in key discussions.

## Updates from the Secretariat

Laurent Chmiel presented three major recent achievements illustrating the impact of OBIS and its community.

The completion of [PacMAN](https://obis.org/2024/11/21/pacman-conclusion-news/), a four-year project co-led by OBIS and funded by the Flanders Government through the UNESCO-Flanders Trust Fund for Science (FUST), granted Fiji the "Marine Invasive Species Monitoring-ready" status, a first among the large ocean states. In the project, OBIS contributed to designing the sampling protocols and creating the [bioinformatics pipeline](https://github.com/iobis/PacMAN-pipeline) as well as the [decision-support tool](https://portal.pacman.obis.org/).

The second achievement was the development of [species distribution maps](https://obis.org/2025/01/23/species-distribution-maps/) for the EU-funded MPA Europe project. This high-resolution marine biodiversity forecasting tool was designed to provide biodiversity foresight for 12,000 species under five IPCC climate change scenarios. The maps will be integrated into the MAP Europe prioritization tool that will provide insights to marine managers and planners on where to implement future marine protected areas.

The third highlight was the successful [UNESCO eDNA Expeditions](https://www.unesco.org/en/edna-expeditions) project, involving 250 young volunteers from 19 countries who collected eDNA marine samples at 21 UNESCO Marine Heritage Sites to assess the state of biodiversity. OBIS coordinated the open-source sampling protocols, sequencing processes, and the development of a [visualization dashboard](https://dashboard.ednaexpeditions.org/), which was showcased at a high-level UNESCO press conference in Paris in December 2024.

Laurent Chmiel presented the opportunities for the Nodes to propose abstracts at IODC-III until 31 January 2025, as well as sessions for the LivingData 2025 conference until 12 February 2025. He also highlighted the [DTO Bioflow call](https://dto-bioflow.eu/second-open-call-marine-biodiversity-data) for data contributions to the EU Digital Twin of the Ocean, with a deadline on 28.2.2025.

Laurent also insisted on the importance of attending the October 2025 OBIS in-person meetings in Bogotá, Colombia (Nodes Coordination Group on 18 October 2025, Data Coordination Group and Products Coordination Group on 19 October 2025 and the OBIS Steering Group on 20 October 2025), as well as the LivingData 2025 conference that will serve as OBIS All Hands Meeting. All OBIS Nodes should fill out [this survey](https://forms.gle/uaELDFs4DFSdf8rx7) to indicate their participation and their need for financial support. Limited financial support is available, attributed in priority to LMIC.

Finally Laurent reminded all Nodes to join the [OBIS Discourse group](https://obis.discourse.group/) to engage in conversations and peer-to-peer exchanges.

## Updates from the OBIS Data Coordination Group

Dan Lear reminded the audience that the OBIS Data Coordination Group (DCG) had its first meeting in December 2024 and its second in January. Ruben Perez from EurOBIS has self-nominated as a co-chair. Dan and Ruben reminded the audience that the DCG is still seeking another co-chair.

Dan and Ruben summarized the first DCG meeting. They explained that the initial December meeting focused on introductions, reviewing the terms of reference, and defining the work plan. Dan and Ruben recapped ongoing discussions on collaboration platforms (Slack, Discourse, etc.), where refinements are needed to define the roles of each tool. Dan and Ruben also touched upon the issue of OBIS data licenses, needed to achieve long-term archival of data. Ruben summarized the second meeting of the Data Coordination Group, outlining three main focus areas moving forward: 1- capacity development and demonstrations-- including showcasing new tools and resources such as a metabarcoding tool for submitting, publishing, and registering DNA-derived data; 2- advancing the work plan by addressing deliverables--such as the OBIS Nodes engagement survey report, updates to OBIS and GBIF DNA-derived data guidelines by volunteers to form subgroups managing specific deliverables; 3- Improving the OBIS-GBIF collaboration, particularly the alignment of controlled vocabularies.

The last update concerns the use of the Humboldt Extension, which OBIS Australia recently discussed in a meeting with GBIF and ALA. The group is exploring how this extension, designed for ecological surveys, can be applied to datasets to reduce redundancies and improve clarity in data documentation.

## Comments on the Flash Talks

Laurent Chmiel asked Johnny Konjarla if the workshop organised by IndOBIS to raise regional awareness about FAIR data principles, data sharing and how to contribute to OBIS could serve as a blueprint for similar , community-led workshops, to which Johnny replied in the affirmative. Dan Lear asked Johnny Konjarla if the Ocean Eyes app (a citizen-science-based app for marine observations available on [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cmlre&hl=en) and [Apple](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/oceaneyes/id6469673451) operating systems) he presented during his flash talk could be extended to other regions, to which Johnny replied that the app is aimed at Indian citizens, with input validation steps that would not be adapted to a scale-up at that stage of development, without excluding a possible extension in a near future.

Dan Lear asked Katherine Tattersall about the way OBIS Australia publishes its data and the difference in the amount of data published on the three main aggregators (Atlas of Living Australia (ALA), OBIS, and GBIF). Katherine replied that OBIS Australia publishes its data at once via its IPT, with the eDNA data limited to ALA and OBIS. Katherine also said that OBIS Australia uses carefully crafted workflows to avoid data duplications while maintaining a publication-at-once process. Dan Lear mentioned interest in sharing best practices on the topic.

Yi Ming Gan (Antarctic OBIS) asked Katherine Tattersall if OBIS Australia, and by extension, the OBIS Secretariat, would develop products based on EOVs, and, if so, if there is a recommendation on the vocabulary to use to facilitate the product uptake. Katherine and Sachit Rajbhandari from OBIS Australia replied by the negative. Pieter Provoost, from the OBIS Secretariat, mentioned work in progress with the GOOS and the BioEco Ocean project to create products based on EOVs, as well as the recommended vocabulary.

After Victor's presentation, Ward Appeltans, OBIS Programme Manager, welcomed OBIS Norway as a new Node and highlighted Ecuador's recent accession to OBIS. He noted that both OBIS and GBIF are recognized in the CBD Global Biodiversity Framework, which requires parties to report to the two organizations the number of recorded species occurrences as part of their national biodiversity commitments. Ward added that the OBIS Secretariat needs to explore how best to support countries in developing meaningful indicators for Target 21 of the Global Biodiversity Framework. The Products Coordination Group is expected to assess how to create a more relevant biodiversity metric and provide guidelines for countries to extract and integrate OBIS data into their national reports.

After Carolina Peralta's flash talk, Ward asked if there could be a possibility for the LAC Nodes to jointly apply to the GBIF's Biodiversity Development Program, to which Carolina replied that she was open to collaboration with other LAC Nodes, and even the US if relevant.

## Guided Discussion

During Session 1, Yi Ming Gan raised the need to better recognize the OBIS community contributions in all OBIS-led actions, to which Pieter replied that ongoing efforts are being deployed in recent products, such as the Species Distribution Maps integrate to the MPA Europe project, to make the Nodes' contribution more visible. Katherine reminded that the OBIS Community benefit from high-level recognition at the IOC and at UNESCO, with strong support for the work accomplished.

Ming suggested developing a Nodes mentorship program with a Node-pairing approach: experienced nodes would mentor newer ones, addressing the challenges that new Nodes face in data mobilization and integration. John Nicholls endorsed the initiative, noting that such a programme needs to be designed around available resources. He suggested an informal approach at first to allow flexibility and testing of different mentoring models. Katherine Tattersall emphasized the importance of a peer-to-peer mentorship approach rather than a rigid, structured program, noting that informal relationships could be especially effective in addressing region-specific challenges. Laurent Chmiel stressed the need to leverage existing OBIS tools, notably the OBIS Discourse Board, to support new nodes and suggested that nodes would share there case studies and best practices. Dan Lear raised concerns about resource allocation and whether nodes could manage mentorship roles while maintaining their own work, recommending that the program start with a small group of volunteers before expanding. The discussion concluded with agreement on an initial pilot phase, focusing on identifying volunteer mentors, matching mentee nodes with experienced ones based on data needs, and documenting best practices for future scaling.

Finally, Ming also suggested increasing automation in OBIS-led processes to bypass the human steps to free OBIS resources from redundant, mundane, and time-greedy tasks. Ming suggested to share best practices but also she suggested the idea of "letters of support" from the OBIS Secretariat to the Nodes to help them secure funding that would benefit to the whole community.

Ward and Ruben noted that the DTO BioFlow call most probably included overseas territories, stressing that the 60,000€ grant per selected institution was a great opportunity for the OBIS Nodes to answer the call.

Stephen Formel (US OBIS) discussed the uncertainty and disruptions caused by the new U.S. administration, stressing that his role as U.S. OBIS Node Manager was already affected. He also noted concerns about federal downsizing, which could impact OBIS management. He suggested that OBIS-SEAMAP and Caribbean OBIS might help support the U.S. OBIS community if needed.
