## Secretariat Updates

Ward Appeltans, OBIS Programme Manager, shared updates from the Secretariat.

BBNJ Agreement  
The BBNJ Agreement, the first international, legally binding instrument covering biodiversity issues beyond national jurisdictions, is poised to enter into force in January 2026. The OBIS Community and the IOC, may have an important role to play in supporting the BBNJ's Clearing House Mechanism, a centralized system that will drive the treaty's implementation and operationalization. OBIS, in particular, is well placed to support the deployment of the four constitutive pillars of the BBNJ Agreement, with concrete and tested yet innovative approaches such as the DOI-based Standardized Batch Identifiers solution proposed by Pieter Provoost. Several regions and countries, including the EU, India, Indonesia, and the Philippines, explicitly recognized IOC and OBIS as key actors for that implementation. OBIS Nodes are warmly encouraged to liaise with their IOC national focal points to ensure OBIS representation in the recently constituted IOC BBNJ Working Group that is waiting for nominations (IOC circular letter ([https://oceanexpert.org/document/37037](https://oceanexpert.org/document/37037)) to Member States seeking nominations).

OBIS Contact page  
It is essential that all Nodes keep their member information up to date on both the [OBIS Contacts](https://obis.org/contact/) page and OceanExpert. Any changes at the Node level, such as new members joining or existing members leaving, should be communicated promptly to the Secretariat, and the corresponding profiles must be updated directly in OceanExpert.

## Data Coordination Group Updates

Katherine Tattersall and Pieter Provoost, OBIS Data Manager, shared updates from the Data Coordination Group.

Assessing OBIS Node Engagement  
The node engagement survey is nearly finalized and will be distributed mid-September, and responses will be collated and visualized immediately. The survey will map node engagement with major international partners such as ODIS, GOOS, GBIF, and TDWG. The results will be reported either at the Data Coordination Group meeting or the Nodes Coordination Group meeting in Bogotá this October, with a possible group exercise there to complement the survey. A report and recommendations will follow later in the year, likely in November or December. The aim is to produce a register of OBIS Node engagement, combining the survey and the Bogotá exercise.

Metadata Synchronisation with ODIS  
Tooling for this is being developed under Marco Bolo's Work Package 1 and is still in the sandboxing stage. Pieter Provoost highlighted that DOIs could be a useful solution for provenance, as they can provide JSON-LD outputs compatible with ODIS. He suggested that OBIS should make more systematic use of DOIs for its products and data exports.

DNA-Derived Data  
A group is meeting regularly to review the DNA section of the OBIS User Manual and identify necessary updates. Active discussions are underway on DNA data extensions and flows, linkages with BOLD, ways to make publishing DNA data easier, the FAIRe checklist developed by Miwa Takahashi, GBIF's MDT tool for eDNA data, and Darwin Core package options. The working document ([https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Y5o\_ExMgQ4gSZeua5ITcg4dx6Nk8Y\_jFj5VytJREJac/edit?usp=sharing](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Y5o_ExMgQ4gSZeua5ITcg4dx6Nk8Y_jFj5VytJREJac/edit?usp=sharing)) is open for comments and new contributions, and group participation is open to all Node members.

Long-Term Archiving of Data  
A survey is underway to assess Nodes' archival capacities. The survey covers accreditation status, data archive size and storage, backup practices and locations, licensing, whether filtering or summarizing is done before submission to OBIS, as well as funding and staffing stability. The survey is expected to launch immediately and remain open for about a month, with results presented in Colombia.

The next meeting of the Data Coordination Group will take place in late September 2025.

## Product Coordination Group Updates

Steve Formel and Jon Pye, the OBIS PCG co-chairs, as well as Silas Principe, OBIS Ecological Modeler, shared updates from the Product Coordination Group.

The Products Coordination Group reported continued progress on the development of the product catalogue. A theme has been prepared for testing, and current efforts are focused on implementing a metadata schema that will be broadly useful across OBIS. Inspiration has been taken from the CEOS data catalogue to align closely with the ODIS specifications for common data exchange conventions such as schema.org. Work on metadata integration towards CKAN and JSON-LD is a critical part of this process, and recent advances are ready to be shared. The prototype theme is now ready for testing, and should be deployed online soon. The development version is available on the OBIS GitHub ([https://github.com/iobis/ckan-docker](https://github.com/iobis/ckan-docker)).

The PCG is also preparing its next meeting for 22 September 2025. This session will include a presentation showcasing how OBIS data, combined with Ocean Tracking Network data, is being applied in Canada to support evaluations of species at risk and marine protected areas. A Shiny app ([https://github.com/dfo-mar-odis/shinySpatialApp](https://github.com/dfo-mar-odis/shinySpatialApp)), available on GitHub, demonstrates how these data products can be used in national reporting and conservation assessments.

## Guided Discussion

OBIS Malaysia's Node Manager, Hafiz Borkhanuddin, raised the issue of facing a large backlog of data and limited staff resources, with only two active staff and two assistants. He asked for advice on accelerating dataset publication. Ward Appeltans advised him to build stronger connections with national biodiversity systems such as MyBIS, insisting that OBIS Nodes provide critical added value in quality control, metadata, and vocabulary alignment, beyond simply formatting data. Katherine Tattersall shared the Australian experience of using summer student internships to publish data and of encouraging partner institutions to provide Darwin Core-ready datasets, which reduces the Node's workload. Ayanda Mahanjana, from AfrOBIS, suggested creating a Technical Advisory Group to pool support from multiple stakeholders. Laurent Chmiel underlined that limited resources are a recurring challenge and stressed the importance of consistent messaging on why OBIS is essential. He recommended continuing this discussion during Steering Group meetings.

Martha Vides, the Node Manager of OBIS Colombia, stressed the importance of following up on the OBIS 2030 strategy and asked if the topic could be addressed at the 13th session of the OBIS Steering Group. She suggested that these could be integrated into the upcoming node survey or discussed within the SG meetings, a point Katherine Tattersall agreed upon. Martha also announced that Colombia would be participating in the next CBD SBSTTA discussions on invasive alien species and asked if OBIS could provide input based on field experience from established monitoring frameworks, such as the one deployed within the PacMAN project. Martha also announced an upcoming meeting in Santa Marta to launch a regional marine invasive species monitoring network using eDNA, with the support of MBON, sparking Katherine to suggest a connection with the OBIS eDNA working group led by Saara Suominen.

Clara Baringo from SiBBr in Brazil introduced a new Darwin Core extension for sociobiodiversity, co-developed with Colombia and Argentina under a CESP project with GBIF. The extension documents species use and knowledge holders, and Clara invited OBIS Nodes to validate vocabularies.

Finally, Andreas Altenburger, the OBIS Norway Node Manager, highlighted the need and the challenge of distinguishing benthic from planktonic organisms in OBIS data, particularly for Arctic benthos assessments. Ward Appeltans acknowledged that this request is recurrent and explained the need for OBIS to tag datasets with benthic and pelagic categories, yet emphasizing that many species change realms throughout their life cycles, making the task complex. He also referred to a recent paper that classified OBIS data as pelagic vs benthic, with the dataset published on Zenodo ([https://zenodo.org/records/15487410](https://zenodo.org/records/15487410)). We have been in contact with the authors to re-create this product and make it available on the OBIS product catalogue.
