## Introduction

The OBIS Products Coordination Group met in two sessions, chaired by Jon Pye and Stephen Formel, with Secretariat support from Silas Principe and Laurent Chmiel. Stephen Formel opened the meeting and explained the objective of the PCG. The agenda covered updates from the Secretariat, two product examples, a review of the PCG's IODE programme deliverables, and a discussion of products that could be published through the catalogue.

## Updates from the Secretariat

Silas Principe showed the current status of the design of the OBIS CKAN instance, which is progressing slowly; Laurent Chmiel will help with a colour palette. Pieter Provoost reported that the server for updating products has been set up with Airflow, and the OBIS datasets will be served through it and updated every few weeks. The Secretariat will work on plugins to improve the CKAN user experience, for example by extracting information directly from GitHub, and proposed creating a GitHub template to standardize how products are reported and organized.

## Product examples

**Globalizing Remora**

**Presented by Bruce Delo, OTN**

Bruce Delo presented a baseline species distribution polygon data product from OBIS that could potentially be integrated into the catalogue. Remora is software developed by IMOS that provides quality control for acoustic telemetry data, but only for Australia, since species distribution models from the Atlas of Living Australia and CAAB currently serve as its QC base. OTN has developed a fork with the goal of making it globally applicable, which requires a dataset of species distributions with global scope.

Because no premade distributions were available, OTN queries OBIS to aggregate data, builds shapefiles on the fly and passes the shapes to Remora, but this takes a long time on the client side. OTN proposed that OBIS create a database or library of polygons that Remora could access, which could itself be a product in the catalogue. Code is available to share: Stephen Formel's code pulls the occurrences, and an R package called voluModel collapses them into minimum convex polygons. Bruce Delo added that OTN is careful to preserve Remora's original function and its applicability for Australian users.

The product could be useful for Silas Principe's current work, and he will contact Bruce Delo to see whether OBIS could operationalize the production of these shapefiles more extensively. The group noted that it could also be a good demonstration for other potential product creators of how to work: having a code base, registering in the catalogue, and using it.

→ Stephen Formel asked whether a provenance chain would be possible for this kind of product. Jon Pye explained that the chain would start with Stephen Formel's code to query OBIS and GBIF, add the voluModel shape-making step, and make clear what assumptions can and cannot be made when using the shape instead of the source datasets, which could be listed as sources in the metadata. Pieter Provoost added that the existing OBIS product aggregating OBIS and GBIF data (https://github.com/iobis/speciesgrids) could resolve some bottlenecks.

**SO-CPR data visualization workflow**

**Presented by Pablo Deschepper**

Pablo Deschepper presented a first attempt at a workflow to advance the visualization of a complex, multilayered product: the Southern Ocean Continuous Plankton Recorder (SO-CPR) (https://github.com/thesnakeguy/SO-CPR_DataViz). The workflow calculates Simpson, Shannon and Hurlbert ES50 indices. Ward Appeltans noted that the `speciesgrids` product developed by Pieter Provoost could speed up part of the workflow.

## Review of IODE programme deliverables

The group reviewed the status of its deliverables under the OBIS work plan:

**Outcome 3: Operational Products Coordination Group.** The indicator of at least five online meetings through October 2025 is in progress, as is the deliverable of sharing meeting minutes through a public portal via the OBIS website. The second indicator is a product work plan for 2026-2027 adopted by SG-OBIS.

**Outcome 4: Enhanced access to FAIR OBIS Products.** The detailed plan for development of the catalogue is in progress, as is the OBIS Products Catalogue deliverable. The remaining indicators are a developmental Products Catalogue stood up for testing, a developmental JupyterHub stood up for testing, and a published production Products Catalogue.

The group noted that progress is needed on some points. A better way to share the meeting minutes needs to be found, and Silas Principe will ask Laurent Chmiel about it. A detailed plan for the Products Catalogue also needs to be developed. The meeting minutes and the catalogue are both due by the end of the year.

**JupyterHub.** Silas Principe noted that the JupyterHub is already functional but needs to be documented; its main purposes are prototyping and education. The group raised concerns about potentially increasing costs given the current financial situation. Pieter Provoost will assess the projected cost, and action will be discussed at the next meeting.

**Indicators for the CBD.** Ward Appeltans stressed that complementary biodiversity indicators are much needed, so that countries can use OBIS to report on their progress against the Global Biodiversity Framework. One existing metric is the number of papers citing OBIS, which OBIS has tracked over the past ten years; another is the number of records available per country. He hoped the group could develop a more useful metric for countries reporting to the CBD, and asked whether a group could be set up within the PCG of people interested in developing such indicators, which he believes deserves high priority.

## Products for the catalogue

Katherine Tattersall asked whether there were already ideas on who might contribute products. Many Nodes produce or have produced data products that could be shared, and the integration of CKAN with ODIS was expected to improve the discoverability of products from the community. Stephen Formel asked members for examples of products from their Nodes that could be published through the catalogue:

- **SCAR (Charlie Plasman):** a Shiny app to help people navigate the metadata of model outputs (https://github.com/SCAR/distant and https://zenodo.org/records/14792295).
- **OBIS Deep Sea:** a product mapping species distributions based on ecosystems and features.
- **Hanieh Saeedi:** an established, ongoing project using OBIS, GBIF and Bio-ORACLE to produce modelling outputs, which could be added.
- **Ward Appeltans:** a recently published pipeline to distinguish benthic from pelagic records in OBIS.

**Derived datasets and citation.** This example prompted a discussion of derived datasets published outside OBIS without full citation of the source datasets. The group agreed it would be better for pipelines like this to be implemented in OBIS, so that users have access to an up-to-date dataset rather than a one-off derived dataset, and saw an opportunity to collaborate with the authors.

The group noted that this kind of situation can arise because OBIS does not mint DOIs as a matter of course. Ward Appeltans explained that OBIS can provide DOIs, but they have to be requested by the data provider, and this is not widely known. The PCG asked whether the ability to generate DOIs could be given to all Nodes. Pieter Provoost has done some work on minting DOIs for derivatives of the full data export. The topic will be taken up at a future PCG meeting.

→ Hanieh Saeedi suggested that the best approach to a benthic/pelagic data product would be for OBIS to integrate the benthic/pelagic information already available in WoRMS into its published data, so that users can retrieve it through OBIS, in the same way as the terrestrial, freshwater and marine flags.

→ Jon Pye suggested a possible partnership with the Ecological Forecasting Initiative (EFI) to help operationalize some OBIS product datasets. EFI runs prediction contests in which model outputs and analysis and forecasting methods compete and are evaluated. He is making first contact with the group and can follow up at future meetings.
