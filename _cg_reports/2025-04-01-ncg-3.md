## Foreword

John Nicholls and Maria Cornthwaite, the OBIS Nodes Coordination Group Co-Chairs, opened the meeting by highlighting ongoing efforts to strengthen communication between Nodes, the Secretariat, and the community, with several ongoing initiatives in development, including a Nodes Assistance platform. The Co-Chairs reminded the participants that thanks to their involvement and dedication, OBIS, as a community-based infrastructure, was part of the very few large, operational global biodiversity data initiatives.

## Steering Group updates

Katherine Tattersall, OBIS Steering Group Co-Chair, confirmed that many OBIS-supported session proposals have been accepted for the [Living Data 2025 conference](https://www.livingdata2025.com/sessions.html). She clarified that the Living Data session approval emails were going out in stages. Session coordinators will have an opportunity to contact potential speakers directly, and additionally most sessions are open for unsolicited submissions. If you have a topic you would like to present on, you can find an appropriate session on the conference website.. The Secretariat will coordinate communication efforts to promote the OBIS-led or OBIS-related sessions and drive interest in abstract submissions (deadline on 20.5.25). Katherine Tattersall reminded the participants that the Living Data conference is a first and that OBIS should be well-represented as a co-organizer, both in the audience and on stage. She reminded the meeting's participants that the early bird registration to the conference was open, with discounts for LMICs. Ward Appeltans reminded the participants that the OBIS coordination group meetings will be held back-to-back with the Living Data Conference in Bogotá in October. He stated that the OBIS Executive Committee is currently working on a solution to avoid an overlap with the GBIF meetings that will take place just before the Living Data conference, answering a question from Yi-Ming Gan (Antarctic OBIS). Martha Patricia Vides Casado discussed the logistics of INVEMAR hosting these OBIS meetings. She emphasized the importance of early confirmation for administrative reasons, especially assistance for participants who need visas from the Colombian government. Ward discussed the need to prepare invitation letters for visa applications. To Luciana Genio (OBIS ISA), who asked if online participation could be possible, Ward responded that in-person attendance is preferred due to the logistical challenges of hybrid meetings but noted that virtual options could be considered, if necessary.

## Secretariat Updates

Ward Appeltans reported on several recent key developments, such as the high-profile [OBIS-led session](https://oceandataconference.org/iodc-iii-programme/) at the 3rd International Ocean Data Conference (IODC-3) in Santa Marta, Colombia and the approval by the [IODE 28th Committee](https://oceanexpert.org/document/36045?utm_medium=email&utm_source=transactional&utm_campaign=oceanexpert_notifications) of the OBIS work plan and budget. He announced that an OBIS Data Officer would start in May, bringing the Secretariat to 9 staff members. Ward announced that a light update of the OBIS website was underway, with the possibility for the OBIS Nodes to give feedback on the changes. He announced that the second phase of the eDNA Expeditions is in the works, with a launch expected to take place at the 2025 UN Ocean Conference in Nice. OBIS will submit a project proposal, "Propagules", to the UNESCO/Flanders Fund-in-Trust for the support of UNESCO's activities in the field of Science (FUST). Propagules aims to develop training materials for university professors and educators to help them teach students about OBIS and how to use its data-related resources in academic settings. Ward finally explained that the ongoing U.S. assessment of their participation in UNESCO might impact the organization's budget, with potential financial arbitrages to come. This might impact OBIS's capacity to sponsor Nodes to participate in the Living Data conference. Ward encouraged everyone to try hard to find funding to attend the OBIS meetings.

## Data Coordination Group (DCG) Updates

Katherine Tattersall and Ruben Perez, DCG Co-Chairs, noted the group's progress on its work plan deliverables, with subgroups formed to lead parallel work. The call for a new DCG Co-Chair remains open to both internal and external candidates until 13.4. Katherine and Ruben thanked Saara Suominen, a scientific officer at OBIS specializing in genetic data, for her workshop on the metabarcoding toolkit, which clarified a particular type of eDNA data publication process. The two Co-Chairs noted the progress of the ongoing mapping efforts to the new Darwin Core – Data Package (DWC-DP) in collaboration with GBIF. Katherine and Ruben requested clear documentation of the OBIS communication tools (Slack, Discourse, email, GitHub) and guidance on each channel's purpose.

## Product Coordination Group (PCG) Updates

Stephen Formel, PCG Co-Chair, reported that the group was organizing its priorities to drive progress. He noted that the JupyterHub cost management is pending further testing. He reminded the meeting's participants of the ongoing migration of OBIS storage from PostgreSQL to Parquet with Apache Airflow to ease product development. Steve also detailed the development of a products catalog via CKAN, with attention to provenance and citation (e.g., DOIs), version control, and minimum metadata standards (Darwin Core, Dublin Core, Schema.org).

## Flash Talk

Yi-Ming Gan (Antarctic OBIS) presented the SCAR Antarctic Biodiversity Portal, a multilingual (English, Spanish, French, Russian) portal for species queries from the Antarctic and Southern Ocean regions. Established in 2005 under the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR), the portal is funded by the Antarctic bioDiVersity dAta iNfrastruCturE (ADVANCE ([https://www.belspo.be/belspo/Fedra/proj.asp?l=en&COD=IM%2FRT%2F23%2FADVANCE](https://www.belspo.be/belspo/Fedra/proj.asp?l=en&COD=IM%2FRT%2F23%2FADVANCE))) project, a 4-year initiative funded by Belgian Science Policy (BELSPO). She outlined the strengths of her team, which spans policy, data, and product development, and the challenges they face due to complex publishing requirements and competition for funding. Ming called for improving the collaboration interface between the OBIS Nodes, GBIF Nodes and the Secretariat, with clearer joint expectations and more forward-looking cooperation. ## Guest speaker

Michal Torma (OBIS Norway / GBIF Norway) introduced "ChatIPT ([https://chatipt.svc.gbif.no/](https://chatipt.svc.gbif.no/))", a chat-based tool using large language models to help map complex datasets to Darwin Core and create Darwin Core Archives. ChatIPT accepts imperfect spreadsheets, provides metadata suggestions, and publishes directly to the GBIF test IPT. He walked attendees through a live demo, showing how users can upload spreadsheets for automated transformation and publication to the GBIF test IPT. The tool, still in the minimum viable product phase, is open for collaboration and contributions. Michal answered several questions from Ana Carolina Peralta Brichtova (Caribbean OBIS), Dimitra Mavraki (MedOBIS), Ruben Perez Perez (EurOBIS), and Katherine Tattersall (OBIS Australia). He explained how users can define custom occurrence IDs, the tool’s current single-file limitation, and its potential to support controlled vocabularies and session memory in the future. Katherine emphasized the importance of tools like ChatIPT and the Metabarcoding Data Toolkit and recommended future discussions on their broader implementation across the GBIF and OBIS communities. Given the interest raised by the demo, a follow-up discussion will take place on the OBIS Discourse board. Michal's presentation is accessible here. \[link to come\].
