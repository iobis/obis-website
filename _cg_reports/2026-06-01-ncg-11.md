## Lightning talks

**Marine Bio-Resource Information System and the OBIS Korea Node***Presented by SangHo Baek (MABIK / OBIS Korea)*SangHo Baek introduced the Marine Bio-Resource Information System (MBRIS), Korea's national platform for the collection, management, and open sharing of marine bio-resources, operated by the National Marine Biodiversity Institute of Korea (MABIK), the country's dedicated national institution for marine biodiversity. As of December 2025, MBRIS manages more than 600,000 records, visualizes verified occurrence data through 14,014 GIS-based maps, and tracks 59 sea turtles in real time. The system also offers an English-language platform, whose Mapper lets users search and visualize marine species occurrence data on an interactive map filterable by year, species, and region. At the same time, the Distribution service allows domestic and international researchers to search and formally request marine biological resources held by MABIK and its partner institutions.

SangHo Baek reminded that MABIK officially joined OBIS as the Korea Node in 2023 and hosted the 12th OBIS Steering Group meeting (15 countries) in 2024. The Node has published 410,029 occurrence records to OBIS. In 2025, the OBIS Korea added to its baseline observation fields (decimalLatitude / longitude, eventDate, institutionCode, occurrenceID, and basisOfRecord) a six-field taxonomic core (kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus) to establish a standardized biological taxonomy, along with samplingProtocol (to control production bias and support replication), lifeStage and sex (to enable demographic analysis of nurseries and spawning grounds), occurrenceStatus (a presence/absence flag that improves Species Distribution Model accuracy), and taxonRank (to optimize search, indexing, and data cleaning). SangHo Baek also reported that, in 2025, Korea launched a cross-sector council gathering the public and private sectors and academia to mobilize fragmented domestic data and standardize and share it through both MBRIS and OBIS.

SangHo Baek flagged one outstanding data integration issue arising from the KIOST-to-MABIK transition: searching OBIS by publisher country (Republic of Korea) returns only 28,634 records, corresponding to the legacy KIOST data, whereas the OBIS Korea Node operated by MABIK holds 410,029 records. The two are currently counted separately, and the Node would welcome help to improve data integration and visibility. (The issue has since been [solved](https://obis.discourse.group/t/node-data-not-linked-to-it-s-corresponding-country/197))

→ **Ward Appeltans** (OBIS Secretariat) congratulated the team, noting that Korea has become an active publisher and an example for the network.

→ **Katherine Tattersall** (OBIS Australia, OBIS SG Co-Chair) congratulated the Nodes on their rapid progress and offered mutual support among the Nodes sharing a similar time zone (Australia, India, Japan, Korea), encouraging them to reach out to each other during overlapping working hours.

→ **Ruben Perez Perez** (OBIS DCG Co-Chair) asked what kind of help would be needed for the data integration issue, and suggested taking it forward either through the Secretariat helpdesk or, if it is a matter of records not being integrated properly, through the Data Coordination Group. He proposed a follow-up exchange on Slack to identify the best route. Laurent Chmiel added that, once SangHo Baek and Ruben Perez Perez find a solution, the discussion and outcome should be saved so that they can be integrated into the future OBIS knowledge base, a valuable use case for it.

Node members are encouraged to contact SangHo Baek for collaborations or further discussion.

## Lightning talk (session 2)

**OBIS ISA: The International Seabed Authority Node***Presented by Luciana Genio (International Seabed Authority / OBIS ISA)*Luciana Genio introduced OBIS ISA, the Node hosted by the International Seabed Authority (ISA), noting that the day-to-day work is carried out by, among other colleagues, Sheldon Carter (database manager), who attended the meeting. The collaboration between ISA and OBIS stems from a 2011 memorandum of understanding between ISA and IOC/IODE recognizing the importance of jointly collecting and exchanging data; the practical work began in 2019. A more recent formal collaboration with ODIS has also been established.The Node's data comes from ISA's exploration contractors. ISA manages 31 contracts with governmental, private, and mixed entities operating across four main regions: the Clarion-Clipperton Zone and the Northwest Pacific (nodules) and the mid-Atlantic and Central Indian basins. Most effort, and the longest history, concerns nodule exploration. Annual reports are received on 31 March, after which data is uploaded to OBIS. Over the past couple of months, the ISA records grew to over 200,000; more are expected following recent contractor submissions.Luciana Genio highlighted the value of OBIS membership for ISA's regional environmental management planning, where basin-scale data inform expert workshops and proposals for area-based protection. The collaboration with WoRMS was singled out as especially active, supporting taxonomic quality, a shared checklist, and forthcoming work aligning the reference database with imagery-based identification. ISA also shares bathymetric data with IHO in line with the Seabed 2030 initiative.Finally, Luciana presented a recently submitted manuscript, co-authored with Leen Vandepitte under ISA's Sustainable Seabed Knowledge Initiative, proposing a taxonomic data quality index that assesses how deep the taxonomic resolution of a dataset goes and whether identifications focus on rare or abundant lineages, with weightings that can be applied. The method was tested on the OBIS ISA Node, and an accompanying app has been developed. Luciana Genio suggested it could be of interest for OBIS products and for BBNJ and other global assessments.

→ **Steve Formel** (OBIS Secretariat, OBIS PCG Co-Chair) thanked Luciana Genio and expressed interest in learning more about ISA's publishing pipeline, which appears to differ from other Nodes, potentially a topic for a future talk. Luciana Genio explained that ISA's DeepData hosts both public environmental data and confidential mineral-resource data, which partly shaped publishing decisions, and noted that DeepData is a relatively recent, evolving system still developing its user-facing and analytical tools. She offered to bring colleagues into a separate, more detailed discussion.

## OTTER: OBIS Training and Technical Exchange Resource (sessions 1 & 2)

Both Co-Chairs led a discussion on OTTER, the node-to-node support programme requested by the NCG and adopted as a 2026 work plan deliverable. OTTER was launched in February 2026, but uptake has been low. A show of hands in both sessions confirmed very limited use. The Co-Chairs launched a discussion to understand obstacles to better uptake: whether the friction lies with the Discourse platform itself, with a lack of clarity on its value, or whether support is already happening effectively through other channels, making OTTER redundant.When asked how they currently seek support, Nodes described a mix of routes: writing directly to the OBIS helpdesk for infrastructure questions; contacting colleagues; raising matters in the relevant Slack channels; or using GitHub for specific issues. Several participants (Katherine Tattersall, Ana Carolina Peralta, Ruben Perez Perez) noted that their lack of consultation habit on the Discourse platform plays a large part in the low uptake.Two structural points emerged repeatedly across both sessions:**\> OTTER has two distinct components.** First, encouraging Nodes to talk to each other and exchange directly; second, capturing the knowledge that results. Laurent Chmiel stressed that Discourse is only one step: The real asset is a curated OBIS knowledge base, hosted by OBIS, and drawing on four streams: the OBIS Discourse, the helpdesk, GitHub, and the Slack channels. **\> A helpdesk-to-Discourse bridge.** Discussed in both sessions (Sachit Rajbhandari, Steve Formel, Katherine Tattersall, Ruben Perez Perez), the idea is to let Node members opt in to having their helpdesk queries posted to Discourse via a dedicated email address, so that conversations are visible, searchable, and archived for the community without requiring everyone to participate. Laurent Chmiel confirmed this is technically possible. Katherine Tattersall cautioned that helpdesk emails are often written quickly and not for a general audience, and suggested an interim editing step, summarizing and, where helpful, expanding acronyms, with the original sender approving the cleaned-up version before publication. Laurent Chmiel confirmed that the knowledge base process already foresees exactly this curation (capture, clean, edit, anonymize, publish), partly via AI and partly by a human, and that the original asker could be brought into the loop to approve the final output.

Several practical Discourse features were clarified in the second session: topics can be closed, and a new "solved" tick box now allows resolved discussions to be harvested in one go for the knowledge base; Discourse can be used almost entirely from email; and the Co-Chairs of the coordination groups can be made admins so they can create and manage their own sections. Laurent Chmiel also reminded participants that the OBIS Discourse is now entirely private; only logged-in Node members can read or participate.Three options were set out for OTTER going forward: - invest further in tutorials and the helpdesk email bridge - redesign OTTER around archiving and making knowledge accessible, treating node-to-node contact as a separate challenge; - Propose an alternative. The Co-Chairs invited continued feedback, including offline, and noted broad agreement that a shared, accessible knowledge base is worth building. A short walk-through (tutorial) on using Discourse, including receiving and posting by email, was identified as a likely next step.

## Data Coordination Group updatesPresented by Ruben Perez Perez and Victor Chocho, OBIS DCG Co-Chair (both sessions)The DCG Co-Chair presented progress on the group's 2026 work plan deliverables and reminded participants that the DCG has open membership and welcomes contributions from across the OBIS community.Two workshops are planned: An eDNA workshop covering the basics is confirmed for 2 July 2026, with morning and evening sessions to reach all time zones; Nodes that have not yet worked with eDNA are encouraged to join. A second workshop on the Darwin Core Data Package (DwC-DP) is in preparation, with discussions under way to deliver it jointly with GBIF teams; dates are still to be confirmed.The taxonomic annotation effort, in which volunteers validate automated annotations against WoRMS, coordinated by the OBIS UK node and supervised by the WoRMS team, has a plan in place. Still, volunteer training has been delayed due to staff changes. The plan is available for review.EOV work is progressing. The aim is to define metrics to identify which OBIS records are eligible to be reported as Essential Ocean Variable data. The plan and workflow are set, and the next subgroup session will run a hands-on session on a pilot case for a couple of EOVs, extracting the Darwin Core information from the EOV specification sheets, transforming it to LinkML, and writing a script to pull the corresponding data from OBIS. Not all EOVs and sub-variables map cleanly to Darwin Core (some require the measurement-or-fact table or NERC controlled vocabularies), so the work starts with a pilot. The subgroup is open to anyone who would like to join the coding.

**Darwin Core Data Package indexing.** The ingestion pipelines have been updated and can now ingest much larger eDNA datasets. Work is also underway to move away from occurrence-centric storage, which creates mass duplication of event-level parameters; discussions with the GBIF Secretariat are planned.**eDNA management course.** A draft of a more comprehensive eDNA guide has been produced, with a website skeleton outlining the topics. It will go well beyond the introductory July workshop and will also serve as a pilot for delivering future training materials in a modular way.**Darwin Core Data Package news (from TDWG).** A very early draft of the DwC-DP usage guide is available. A new experimental tool, the [Darwin Core Data Package Designer](https://gbif.github.io/dwc-dp/designer/) (created by John Wieczorek and colleagues), lets users select the tables and fields needed to transform their raw data into a Darwin Core Data Package, indicates how the tables relate, and outputs an empty package skeleton with the tables, columns, and JSON file ready to fill, a useful starting point for those new to the format.

## Product Coordination Group updatesPresented by Silas Principe, OBIS Secretariat (first session) and Steve Formel, OBIS Secretariat and PCG Co-Chair (second session)The OBIS Products Catalog is ready to launch. A production instance is live at products.obis.org, and Silas Principe has revamped the aesthetic in a development version. Existing products will be cleared, and the catalog repopulated in a phased approach, starting with a small number of example products from the PCG that demonstrate the expected level of quality and documentation, then moving to products from the Nodes, and later opening to the broader community. A governance document, setting out which product types to expose and the minimum requirements for quality, maintenance, and metadata, is in draft and being finalized. National pilot product (Outcome 8): This national pilot product will show how OBIS can serve national policy outcomes. Over the past six months, the group reviewed examples of OBIS-based products, including OBIS Australia's seabird data product built at the request of a government department, and the Allen Coral Atlas / Artificial Light At Night (ALAN) database used across regions and institutions. At its most recent meeting, the PCG expressed strong interest in a "one-button" function for Nodes to report CBD metrics from OBIS, which are named in CBD targets 20 and 21, drawing on existing experience from Nodes such as OBIS Ecuador. The aim is to build an easy solution, then have the PCG respond to the proof of concept, with results presented at the steering group meeting in the autumn. If Nodes have a recurring national-reporting question that is always difficult to answer from OBIS, the PCG would welcome hearing about it to help target this outcome.

## Steering Group updatesPresented by Katherine Tattersall (first session) and Dan Lear (second session), OBIS SG Co-ChairsThe SG has been organizing the 8th OBIS Executive Committee (EC-8) meeting, scheduled online for 15 June 2026. The Executive Committee is made up of the Secretariat together with the Co-Chairs of the Steering Group and of the Coordination Groups, and meets around mid-year as a check-in on progress against the work plan, surfacing both challenges and successes. The EC will also begin planning work plans for the coming period and coordinate OBIS communication around upcoming international events, including the World Conference on Marine Biodiversity (WCMB) and the International Marine Data and Information Systems conference (IMDIS).

A change to the usual meeting cadence was flagged in both sessions. To align with the wider IOC and IODE calendar, the IODE-29 session has been brought forward to 5–9 October 2026, ahead of the OBIS Steering Group meeting at which the work plan and budget would normally be set. As a result, an additional online pre-steering group meeting will be held to define the OBIS work plan and budget so they can feed into IODE-29 for approval. All Node representatives will be asked to attend this shorter online meeting. After this adjustment, OBIS meetings should fall into a seamless matching cadence with the regular IODE and IOC meetings.The in-person Steering Group meeting will take place from 11 to 16 November 2026 in Ostend, Belgium, back-to-back with the World Conference on Marine Biodiversity. The longer format, including the weekend, is intended to give the Co-Chairs more time to process inputs before adopting decisions and the work plan, a lesson learned from the compressed schedule in Bogotá. This will also give the opportunity for workshopping, training, and possibly a field trip. Leen Vandepitte warmly invited the OBIS community to WCMB 2026. The Co-Chairs encouraged Nodes attending this and other events to get in touch, both for OBIS support materials and to coordinate who will be present.
