## Introduction

The first meeting of the OBIS Products Coordination Group (PCG) was chaired by Stephen Formel, with Secretariat support from Silas Principe, and hosted on MS Teams by OBIS-USA. The agenda covered introductions and members' hopes for the group, updates from the Secretariat, a review of the purpose of the PCG as set out at EC-6, the resources members could contribute, and the Products Catalogue.

## What members hope the PCG will achieve

Participants introduced themselves in the context of OBIS and described what they hoped the group would achieve. The hopes expressed included:

- more tools relating to South Africa and the southern African region, to better understand biodiversity patterns
- data recovery
- showcasing products from the community, and making tools that can be contributed back to it
- ground truthing of tracking data
- near real-time and real-time data products
- making sure products are properly documented
- capacity development on OBIS products
- products that help manage seabed areas monitored by the ISA
- learning from each other's expertise
- understanding which products already exist, the standards they use, and developing new products, especially new data pipelines
- showcasing regional data
- synergies between the PCG and the DCG
- tools that help with reporting to international conventions and against national targets
- products that make data transformation and standardization easier, helping professionals in natural history collections to share their data openly

Members fell broadly into three groups: those using the PCG as a touch point for OBIS, to listen and learn; those wishing to contribute but without specific ideas yet; and those wishing to contribute with specific ideas.

## Updates from the Secretariat

**Indicators for the CBD.** OBIS is referred to in the CBD target indicators. Ward Appeltans suggested the group could help develop indicators and other products that help countries monitor and report to the CBD, going beyond a simple count of records. Victor Chocho and Hanieh Saeedi highlighted the importance of such indicators and tools as a source of information for BBNJ, CMS, CITES and IPBES.

**Project results.** Ward Appeltans reported on the final results of eDNA Expeditions (dashboard: https://dashboard.ednaexpeditions.org/), which was presented at a press conference, and of PacMAN (https://portal.pacman.obis.org/), which held a successful final meeting in Fiji. Both can be developed further and promoted. Recent results for MPA Europe (https://shiny.obis.org/distmaps), on which Silas Principe is working, have also been positive: the European Commission was very impressed with the distribution maps and highlighted the project's success in achieving the FAIR principles.

## Purpose of the PCG

Members were reminded of the purpose of the PCG as set out at EC-6. The EC-6 document records where the group has come from and which tasks were identified as priorities.

## Identifying resources

The EC-6 notes proposed that the first PCG meetings should be used to identify the scope of resources available to carry out the group's actions, including how much time Nodes can contribute outside meetings, and whether Nodes have compute or other resources to share. This item was folded into the Products Catalogue discussion. OTN uses GeoServer, and nationally CKAN, and can share expertise in both; it can also teach Carpentries courses on topics such as Git and GitHub. VLIZ also uses GeoServer and can share its expertise.

## Products Catalogue

The EC-6 notes identified the Products Catalogue as a clear priority, to inform users about the existing data products supported by OBIS.

**JupyterHub and CKAN.** Silas Principe reviewed the status of the infrastructure on behalf of Pieter Provoost. OBIS runs a JupyterHub (https://jupyter.obis.org/), and Pieter Provoost has proposed CKAN as the catalogue platform (https://ckan.obis.org/). The idea is not to show the same data as OBIS but the products derived from it; for now the instance holds only a few examples, such as the gridded product and the full OBIS export.

→ Jon Pye noted that oceanographic data providers in Canada also use CKAN, and that CKAN is stackable: for example, the CIOOS Atlantic catalogue (https://catalogue.cioosatlantic.ca/en) nests into the national CIOOS catalogue (https://catalogue.cioos.ca/). Hanieh Saeedi added that Senckenberg uses CKAN for its research data management metadata.

→ Salvador Fernandez said VLIZ has experience using GeoServer with data products and was happy to discuss it. Jon Pye explained that GeoServer is an OGC-standard geospatial data server, and that OTN runs one too.

**Proposed infrastructure.** All code lives on GitHub. Products are developed in the JupyterHub and, once in good shape, move to Airflow, an Apache technology for scheduling runs of code from GitHub. Airflow produces the products in a data lake, from which they are catalogued in CKAN and GeoServer.

## Next steps

The group worked through a set of open questions:

- **Who will have access to CKAN, for example Nodes?** No answer was recorded.
- **Will data products be validated?** Validation is considered a good idea but has not yet been designed. Ward Appeltans added that only validated products can be advertised.
- **Will the previous GitHub repository (https://github.com/iobis/iwg-products) be used?** This is open and up to the group. There are already several repositories for different parts of the infrastructure, including https://github.com/iobis/jupyterhub-deploy-docker/tree/obis, https://github.com/iobis/obis-hub and https://github.com/iobis/iwg-products. Jon Pye suggested providing guidance on how Nodes can stand up their own versions of the repositories. Roughly half to two-thirds of the group is comfortable with GitHub.
- **What is for the group and what is for the Secretariat?** The general idea is that the group leads, with Secretariat support. The main technical developments, such as CKAN and the JupyterHub, would be implemented by the Secretariat. It is important for the group to set out its ideas and direction, and the Secretariat can help decide what is feasible in terms of technical and financial resources. Stephen Formel asked whether what is needed has been documented. Silas Principe said Pieter Provoost had previously prepared an overview of costs, which will be checked to see whether it is still up to date.
- **Is there a list of preferred formats for data products, such as GeoTIFF or NetCDF?** Silas Principe said that, more than any particular format, what matters is that formats are cloud-based and performant, such as Parquet and COGeoTIFF. Katherine Tattersall stressed the importance of aiming for interoperability.
- **Is cloud technology accessible to everyone, and are there resources or infrastructure the group should avoid because they would limit users?** Katherine Tattersall noted the considerable experience in the group and suggested adding a table for members to record it. No one had yet named a technology to avoid.
- **Do members have access to the JupyterHub?** Not explicitly yet: Pieter Provoost currently has to add accounts on request by e-mail. Some members have accounts from a workshop held the previous year. The landing page is https://jupyter.obis.org/.

→ Ben Donnelly described how his team approaches data downloads and packaging, for example asking users before they download whether they would like to discuss the data or have any questions, to get a better understanding of why people use the data. Jon Pye noted that data consultations involve a balance between consultation and open, FAIR data: it is valuable to hear how data is being used, and knowing why is very useful for funders, but putting up barriers often turns people away. Ben Donnelly agreed, stressing that the aim is not to create barriers but to understand better what makes an ideal data product for different users.
