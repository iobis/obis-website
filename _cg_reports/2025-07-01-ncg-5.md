## Secretariat Updates

Ward Appeltans shared updates from the Secretariat.

OBIS reaches 161M occurrence records!  
OBIS reached 161 million occurrence records, thanks in part to recent large data harvests. Just one day after announcing 141 million records, OBIS added over 20 million new records, mainly from the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA)/MGNIFY. Despite delays in MGNIFY’s data export pipeline, OBIS decided not to wait for a “perfect” export and proceeded with harvesting, acknowledging it can be updated later.

eDNA Expeditions launch at UNOC3  
OBIS successfully secured funding from the Minderoo Foundation (Australia) to launch Phase II of the eDNA Expeditions at UNOC3. While Phase I was built as a single campaign, Phase II will establish time series of observations, based on 6–9 sampling campaigns per site. In Phase 2, eligible sites are no longer limited to World Heritage Sites and can include other MPAs. Node involvement as a selection criterion: OBIS Nodes are encouraged to be directly involved, and site connection to a Node will be considered in site selection to ensure data quality and community engagement. A new survey will be launched at the end of August 2025 to select sites.

Increasing OBIS eDNA capacity  
Additionally, a potential US$3 million Belgian-funded project is under discussion. This initiative could fund the purchase of automated eDNA samplers, improve OBIS’s bioinformatics pipelines, support training material development, and transfer equipment to developing countries to build capacity. Funds must be spent by May 2026, putting pressure on planning and implementation.

OBIS-UK is a non-funded supporting partner in their application.

## Data Coordination Group Updates

Ruben Perez shared updates from the Data Coordination Group.

Essential Ocean Variables (EOVs) to Darwin Core Transformations  
OBIS collaborates with GOOS to determine which EOV schemas already have transformations into Darwin Core and which still need to be addressed. This will avoid duplication of effort and ensure standardization. Once this inventory is complete, OBIS will develop ready-to-use transformation scripts for the community.

Assessing OBIS Node Engagement  
A Miro board activity will soon be shared with Nodes in an upcoming meeting to evaluate their engagement with key global and regional strategic partners, particularly those who have working groups on data standards and implementations. This assessment will serve as a point-in-time snapshot, though it may be an annually recurring report. Nodes are encouraged to respond in session, though there may be a 1:1 follow-up with some Nodes.  Node input will help OBIS map network involvement more clearly.

Metadata Synchronisation with ODIS  
OBIS core metadata is already flowing to ODIS, meeting a key objective (Deliverable 4 of the DCG workplan). Further improvements are underway to expand the metadata fields, ensuring richer information is visible and consistent.

DNA-Derived Data Strategy Shift  
The DCG decided to shift from updating the “OBIS–GBIF DNA-derived data guidelines” to enhancing internal capacity development within OBIS Nodes. This new approach includes:  
Updating the OBIS Manual to include details on using event core with occurrence and DNA extensions  
Strengthening Node capacity to process and share such data,  
Fostering more internal understanding and consistency across the network.

Long-Term Archiving of Data  
OBIS will assess how individual Nodes manage long-term archiving of datasets, scripts, and derived products. A survey or living document (format still to be decided) will be circulated soon to gather strategies currently used by Nodes. This will inform whether a recommended common approach for archiving is needed.

Darwin Core Data Package Implementation  
OBIS is actively contributing to discussions on the Darwin Core Data Package, developed in collaboration with GBIF and TDWG. The focus is on how OBIS will index and integrate datasets submitted in this format and developing documentation and user guidance aligned with GBIF’s recommendations. Since the official launch of the data package is expected in early 2026, the coming months offer a critical window for OBIS to define its internal approach.

Controlled Vocabularies (Vocabs) Work  
The OBIS Vocabulary Group is testing atomic-based vocabulary codes compatible with the Darwin Core Data Package. These can potentially be reconstructed into the more traditional PCR-style codes used by OBIS. Though technical, community members interested in this work were encouraged to join the Vocabulary Group discussions.

Leadership Update  
Ruben announced he will be leaving the Flanders Marine Institute (VLIZ) and EurOBIS for GBIF Spain. While stepping down from his current institutional role, Ruben hopes to remain active within the OBIS network, depending on his future arrangements. Discussions are ongoing about whether he will continue as DCG Co-Chair or in another capacity.

## Node-to-Node Support Programme

Katherine Tattersall introduced a proposed capacity development framework designed to facilitate structured peer-to-peer support between OBIS Nodes. Unlike traditional training or mentorship models, this initiative centers on Nodes helping each other in a flexible, scalable, and community-owned way.

Goals and rationale of the programme  
- Address skill gaps and specific technical challenges in some OBIS Nodes.  
- Offer structured support by Nodes, for Nodes, leveraging shared experience and community knowledge.  
- Minimize the burden on trainer Nodes by standardizing messaging and materials.  
- Provide recognition and visibility for those who contribute their expertise.

How it works  
1- Needs and Capacity Mapping via a survey (or live session at the next NCG meeting) to identify Nodes’ support needs, map the capacities and expertise available across the network  
2- A small coordination team (OBIS Sec + Nodes members) will handle matchmaking between needs and capacities  
3- Sessions will be flexible, based on topics, availability, time zones, and languages and will follow needs and capacity analysis, and may take the form of small-group workshops or one-on-one sessions.  
4- Content development will be coordinated by the OBIS Secretariat and delivered by OBIS Nodes members, according to availability and resources, with a focus on quality, coherence, and consistency across training.  
5- Sessions will be documented, summarized and published in a FAQ/knowledge base on obis.org ([http://obis.org](http://obis.org)). Trainers will be formally acknowledged via, for example: a dedicated certification or badge (e.g., on OceanExpert), recognition on the OBIS website and social media, or other options. The recognition component is very much open to suggestions from the Community.

Benefits  
For Nodes receiving support:  
- Technical upskilling and problem-solving support  
- Focused capacity development

For Nodes offering support:  
- Formal recognition of staff expertise through certification or digital badge and acknowledgement on the OBIS website.  
- Promotion through OBIS communication channels that can be leveraged to demonstrate Node influence for funders, hosting institutions, and others.

This framework remains a proposal, and feedback from the OBIS Nodes Coordination Group (NCG) is actively encouraged. Katherine concluded by thanking Elizabeth Lawrence (OBIS Secretariat) and Yi Ming Gan (Antarctic OBIS) for their key roles and their precious input shaping the proposal.

## Transformational Alignment Initiative (TAI)

Laurent Chmiel presented a proposal developed in response to a request from the UN Ocean Decade Coordination Office, aiming to significantly increase biodiversity data contributions to OBIS.

### Background and Rationale

The UN Decade Coordination Office for Ocean Observing contacted OBIS to explore concrete actions to boost data contributions, especially biodiversity-related data, without overburdening the Nodes. OBIS responded by co-developing a proposal to expand its contributor base through a train-the-trainer model, leveraging community knowledge while addressing capacity limitations across OBIS Nodes.

Challenges and potential solution  
At present, OBIS Nodes conduct one-on-one or group training for potential contributors within their local networks. While effective, this method is limited by finite human and institutional capacity, restricted geographical or linguistic reach, and the strain on already busy Nodes.

Proposed Solution: the Train-The-Trainer approach: A Scalable Training Architecture based on a three-tiered model. This train-the-trainer approach multiplies OBIS’s reach while respecting the bandwidth and autonomy of each Node.

Super Trainers (OBIS Nodes): Volunteer Nodes act as “super trainers,” delivering structured training to a new group of intermediaries called OBIS Data Coaches.

OBIS Data Coaches: Community-based, often outside the direct reach of current Nodes. Volunteers. They receive standardized training and toolkits and are empowered to train local contributors, identify local data holders, and facilitate data publication to OBIS.

Potential contributors: identified by the OBIS Data Coaches or the Nodes. Receive the training enabling them to contribute to OBIS.

### TAI: Key Principles

Voluntary participation: No Node is obligated to participate.

Recognition and motivation: Trainers and coaches may be acknowledged via badges, certifications, …

Agility and pragmatism: Implementation is iterative, not prescriptive, and seeks synergies with other initiatives.

### An implementation in two phases

Phase 1: Design and Setup  
Needs assessment to identify gaps in data contribution across communities.

Recruitment of volunteer Nodes willing and able to act as super trainers.

Creation of toolkits and curricula, building on existing OBIS resources (e.g., OceanTeacher materials, OBIS manual, multilingual modules), taking into account and using existing material

Phase 2: Rolling Implementation

Training of selected OBIS Data Coaches.

Matchmaking between trained coaches and communities needing support.

Ongoing monitoring, feedback loops, and recognition mechanisms for trainers and coaches.

### Next Steps

Call for volunteer Nodes to serve as super trainers (contact Laurent directly).

Launch of needs assessment and toolkit design.

Begin rollout when ready, with no imposed timeline to ensure alignment with Node capacity.
