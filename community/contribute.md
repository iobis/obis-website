# Contribute to OBIS

OBIS is a marine biodiversity data platform and a community: it relies on the contributions and involvement of individuals and institutions to grow. We have listed below six potential entry points to contribute to OBIS, become an OBIS Node, or support the Community. If you do not find a path that suits you or if you need additional information, send the OBIS Secretariat a message at [helpdesk@obis.org](mailto:helpdesk@obis.org).

## Contribute to OBIS with marine biodiversity data

Transition from local marine biodiversity data holder to becoming a global contributor: make your data available on OBIS! We welcome data contributors from all regions and backgrounds, whether you are a marine biologist, part of a privately funded project, an institution, or a citizen science initiative. Beyond marine occurrence data, you can contribute to OBIS with numerous data types, such as, for example, presence and absence data, abiotic and biotic measurements, or DNA-derived data, including DNA sequences.

To publish a dataset to OBIS, there are five main steps you must go through:

1. Find a suitable OBIS Node
2. Structure your data
3. Format your dataset into Darwin Core
4. Have your dataset undergo quality control checks
5. Publish your data via an OBIS Node's Integrated Publishing Toolkit

You will receive support and guidance at each step of the process. Read more about how to contribute to OBIS with marine biodiversity data: [https://obis.org/data/contribute/](https://obis.org/data/contribute/)

## Cite OBIS data

Are you using OBIS data? Cite it! You will make data providers more visible and their contributions properly acknowledged. Data citation creates a virtuous cycle that strengthens the entire global marine biodiversity data chain. Learn how to cite OBIS data: [https://manual.obis.org/citing.html](https://manual.obis.org/citing.html)

## Become an OBIS Node

Becoming an OBIS node shows your organization's commitment to making marine biodiversity data collected by multiple organisations in your region or within a thematic scope openly, durably, and globally accessible. By becoming an OBIS Node, you join a community of +30 nodes dedicated to advancing ocean science, developing data standards, improving data quality processes, and supporting capacity development where it is most needed.

An OBIS Node is either an [Associate Data Unit (ADU) or a National Oceanographic Data Centre (NODC)](https://iode.org/about/community/) under the International Oceanographic Data and Information Exchange (IODE). A Node can be hosted by one or more institutions or organizations and contributes to mobilizing and publishing marine biodiversity data into OBIS. A Node can be regional or national (representing a region or a country), or thematic (focusing on a specific area of interest or technology type).

If the applying institution is already an NODC or an ADU, the application process consists of sending a letter to the OBIS Secretariat expressing interest in becoming an OBIS Node. If the applying institution is neither an ADU nor a NODC, the application process consists of applying to set up an ADU first, with a specific role as an OBIS Node. Any project, programme, institution, or organization handling marine biodiversity data can apply to become an ADU, provided it can demonstrate responsibility in data stewardship: a credible plan to manage and preserve that data over the long term, institutional backing (letters of support), a sustained commitment beyond short-term goodwill, and willingness to work alongside an NODC and the OBIS community. Read the full application process to become an ADU under IODE: [https://iode.org/faq-items/how-to-set-up-an-iode-associate-data-unit-adu/](https://iode.org/faq-items/how-to-set-up-an-iode-associate-data-unit-adu/)
